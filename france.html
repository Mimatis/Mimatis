After the loss of a baby in France

Clear, checked information for parents after a miscarriage, a stillbirth, a termination for medical reasons or the death of a baby soon after birth: your rights, the paperwork, funerals, leave from work and where to find support. French terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

Checked in October 2026. Not legal or medical advice. Your hospital team, town hall (mairie) or health insurance fund (CPAM) can confirm your situation.

If you are in danger or thinking about ending your life, call 3114 (free, 24 hours a day, also for family members), or 15 / 112 in an emergency.

Four numbers that shape your rights

15 weeks

From the end of week 15, the birth can be recorded at the town hall, with your baby’s name.

22 weeks

From week 22, or a weight of 500 g, maternity and partner leave apply as after a live birth.

14 days

to decide whether you arrange the funeral yourselves or leave it to the hospital.

Day 1

After a miscarriage before week 22, sick pay starts on the first day of leave.

French hospitals count weeks from the first day of your last period (semaines d’aménorrhée, SA). This is about two weeks more than weeks since conception. All weeks on this page are counted this way.

The rules on registration, names and funerals are the same after a termination for medical reasons (interruption médicale de grossesse, IMG).

Early miscarriage, before 15 weeks

Miscarriage in the first months is common, and it is still the loss of a pregnancy and of the child you were imagining.

  • Depending on your situation, the miscarriage may happen naturally, with medication, or with a short procedure. Your doctor or midwife will explain the options.
  • There is no birth record at this stage, and the hospital takes care of the remains.
  • If you need time off, your doctor or midwife can give you sick leave (arrêt maladie). Since 1 January 2024 it is paid from the first day, with no waiting days. This covers employees, civil servants and the self-employed.
  • Since September 2024, each region offers a care pathway after miscarriage (parcours fausse couche) with doctors, midwives and psychologists, open to you and your partner. Ask your doctor or midwife.

Later loss, 15 to 21 weeks

At this stage you usually give birth to your baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after a later loss. See at the hospital.

  • The doctor or midwife gives you a medical certificate of birth (certificat médical d’accouchement). With it, you can ask the town hall to record your baby with first names and a family name. See giving your baby a name.
  • You have 14 days to decide whether to arrange the funeral yourselves or to leave it to the hospital. See funerals.
  • Sick leave is paid from day 1. Your employer cannot dismiss you for 10 weeks after a miscarriage between week 14 and week 21, except for serious misconduct or a reason unrelated to the pregnancy.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks or 500 g

From this point, French law recognises the birth for leave and family benefits, even though your baby was not born alive.

  • You can record your baby at the town hall, with names. See giving your baby a name.
  • You have 14 days to decide about the funeral. See funerals.
  • The mother is entitled to maternity leave (congé maternité), which starts from the birth. Your health insurance fund (CPAM) confirms the exact length for your situation.
  • The partner is entitled to birth leave, paternity leave and bereavement leave. See leave from work.
  • The family allowance fund (CAF) pays a one-off allowance after the death of a child. See money.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby lived, even for a few minutes, the town hall issues a birth certificate and a death certificate, at any stage of pregnancy. The doctor confirms that your baby was born alive. Your baby has a legal name and is part of your family record. A funeral (burial or cremation) is required and is arranged by the family. Both parents have full leave rights.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is an emergency, you do not need to decide quickly. You can ask the team to explain each step, as many times as you need.

You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, to see and hold your baby, alone or with family, including brothers and sisters.
  • A room away from the maternity ward, where you will not hear other newborns.
  • Photos, taken by you or by the staff. Volunteer photographers from Souvenange can come to the maternity unit; ask the midwife.
  • Keepsakes: hand and foot prints, the identity bracelet, a lock of hair, the blanket.
  • To understand why. Tests on the placenta and an examination of your baby (autopsie) can sometimes explain what happened. An examination of your baby is only done with your written consent.
  • A ceremony or blessing, through the hospital chaplaincy (aumônerie), for any faith or none.

None of this is required. Parents choose differently, and there is no right way.

Giving your baby a name

From the end of week 15, if you wish, your baby can be recorded at the town hall (mairie).

  • The hospital gives you a medical certificate of birth (certificat médical d’accouchement).
  • With it, you can ask the town hall of the place of birth for an acte d’enfant sans vie, a record of a child born without life.
  • You may give your baby first names and a family name (since the law of 6 December 2021). Both are optional.
  • There is no deadline. You can do this after you leave hospital, or much later.
  • The record can be added to your family book (livret de famille). If you are not married and this is your first child, you can ask for a family book.
  • The record has no legal effect on inheritance or parentage. It is an official recognition that your baby existed, and it is needed for some benefits and leave.

Before week 15, no certificate is issued and there is no town hall record.

Saying goodbye: funerals

From week 15, you have 14 days after the birth to choose:

  • To arrange the funeral yourselves, through a funeral director (pompes funèbres) of your choice: burial or cremation, with a ceremony if you wish. You need the acte d’enfant sans vie. Ask for a written quote.
  • To leave it to the hospital, which then arranges a cremation. The hospital must wait until the 14 days have passed. Ask the team what happens next, and whether you can be told the date and place.

Many towns have a section of the cemetery for babies (carré des anges or carré des bébés). Ask your town hall.

If your baby was born alive, a burial or cremation is required and is arranged by the family.

  • Paternity leave (congé de paternité et d’accueil de l’enfant) also applies to a second mother. It cannot be taken at the same time as bereavement leave.
  • Leave for the death of a child (congé pour décès d’un enfant, 14 working days) is paid by the employer. Bereavement leave (congé de deuil, 8 days) can be split into two parts and taken within a year; it is paid by health insurance. Both apply from week 22 or 500 g, and each employed parent can take them.
  • Your collective agreement (convention collective) may give more days. Your HR department or CPAM can confirm the dates.

Money and benefits

  • Allowance after the death of a child (allocation forfaitaire en cas de décès d’un enfant): paid by the CAF (or MSA for farm workers) when a baby dies from the 20th week of pregnancy, which is week 22 in the French count. In 2026 it is about €2,300, or half that above an income limit. Send the CAF the acte d’enfant sans vie or the death certificate.
  • Birth grant (prime à la naissance): under an income limit, it can be paid if the birth happens from the 6th month of pregnancy.
  • Depending on where you live, the CAF may also help with funeral costs or offer help at home. Ask to speak to a CAF social worker (travailleur social).

When you go home

  • Your body may react as after any birth, including milk coming in. Ask your midwife about this before you leave, and see a midwife or doctor if anything worries you.
  • Psychological support: with Mon soutien psy, up to 12 sessions a year with a partner psychologist are reimbursed. Your doctor or midwife can refer you. The regional miscarriage pathway also includes psychologists.
  • Your partner may grieve differently and more quietly. Each of you can ask for support.
  • Brothers and sisters understand more than adults often expect. Short, honest words help, and so does including them in remembering. Nos Tout-Petits runs workshops for siblings.
  • Some days are harder: the due date, anniversaries, family celebrations. In France, 15 October is the day of awareness for pregnancy and infant loss, with ceremonies in many towns.
  • A next pregnancy can bring new worry. Several associations offer groups for this.
