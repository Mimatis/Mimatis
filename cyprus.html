After the loss of a baby in Cyprus

Information for parents in the Republic of Cyprus after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, documents, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Cyprus publishes very little official guidance on parents’ rights after a stillbirth, so this page tells you what to ask. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, the Social Insurance Services and your employer.

In an emergency, call 112. If you need someone to talk to, the Cyprus Samaritans answer 8000 7773, free, every day from 16:00 to midnight.

Four things to know

Ask

Cyprus has little published guidance on rights after a stillbirth. Ask the hospital midwife and the Social Insurance Services what applies to you.

22 weeks

Maternity leave for a first or second birth is 22 weeks (since March 2024). Ask how it applies after a stillbirth.

A funeral

Stillborn babies can be buried in a cemetery, in agreement with the church. Ask your parish priest.

Not alone

Birth Forward, a Cypriot charity, lists support for parents after pregnancy loss.

Whatever the paperwork says, your baby and your loss are real.

Your situation, step by step

Whether your loss counts legally as a miscarriage (αποβολή) or a stillbirth (θνησιγένεια) depends on how far the pregnancy had come, and this affects documents and benefits. Ask your doctor which applies, and write it down. If your baby showed any sign of life, at any stage, your baby was born alive.

Miscarriage

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you a sick-leave certificate.
  • Saying goodbye: ask the hospital what is possible for your baby, and tell them what you would like, as early as you can.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth

When a baby dies before birth later in pregnancy, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Documents: ask the hospital which certificate you will receive, and ask the civil registry whether your baby’s name can be recorded.
  • A funeral: stillborn babies can be buried in a cemetery in agreement with the church. Ask the hospital and your parish priest. See saying goodbye.
  • Mother: ask the Social Insurance Services whether maternity leave and maternity allowance apply after your baby’s birth, and for how long.
  • Partner: ask your employer and the Social Insurance Services whether paternity leave applies.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are both registered. A funeral follows. Ask the Social Insurance Services about maternity and paternity leave and allowances in your situation.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the name band. If the hospital does not offer them, ask, or take photos yourself.
  • To name your baby, dress them, and have family meet them.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge. Public hospitals have a patients’ rights officer (Λειτουργός Δικαιωμάτων Ασθενών) who can help.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • Burial: stillborn babies can be buried in a cemetery, in agreement with the church. A funeral director or your parish priest can help.
  • Ask the hospital what happens if you do not arrange a burial yourself.
  • Other faiths: ask your own religious community or a funeral director about the options.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • Ask the hospital which certificate you will receive after a miscarriage or a stillbirth.
  • Ask the civil registry whether your baby’s name can be recorded.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, both the birth and the death are registered, with your baby’s name.

Whatever the register says, you can name your baby in your family, at the funeral and in our memorial garden.

  • Maternity leave in Cyprus is 22 weeks for a first or second birth and 26 weeks from the third (since March 2024).
  • The Social Insurance Services handle maternity and paternity allowances. Ask them in writing, so you have the answer on record.
  • If you are not ready to work, talk to your doctor about sick leave.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Find support: Birth Forward keeps a directory of psychologists and groups in Cyprus who support parents after pregnancy loss, and has run a loss-in-pregnancy support group.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call 116 111 (weekdays 12:00–20:00). A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and baby loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
