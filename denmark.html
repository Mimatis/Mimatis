After the loss of a baby in Denmark

Checked information for parents in Denmark after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, your baby’s name, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your parish office or Udbetaling Danmark. Danish terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live, call Livslinien on 70 201 201, anonymously.

Your situation, step by step

In Denmark, the line is 22 weeks of pregnancy. A baby born without signs of life from 22+0 weeks is stillborn (dødfødt). A loss before that counts as a miscarriage or late miscarriage (abort, sen abort). If your baby showed any sign of life, at any stage, your baby was born alive.

Bereavement leave with benefits depends on your work situation. Udbetaling Danmark decides.

Miscarriage or late loss, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your GP or hospital doctor can sign you off sick, with sickness benefit (sygedagpenge). Bereavement leave does not apply before 22 weeks.
  • Saying goodbye: many hospitals let you bury your baby yourself, or arrange a burial in a shared grave. There are no national rules, so practice differs. Ask the hospital what is possible, and take the time you need to decide.
  • A name: you can give your baby a name. It can be written in the parish register (kirkebogen) together with a burial or a blessing.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 22 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Your baby receives an administrative CPR number.
  • A name: a stillborn baby cannot be baptised or formally named, but a name of your choice (kælenavn) can be written in the parish register.
  • Burial or cremation: the hospital gives you the papers, and a funeral director can help you. See saying goodbye.
  • Both parents: up to 26 weeks of bereavement leave (sorgorlov) with benefits, from the day after the birth.
  • Mother: if you are still unwell because of the pregnancy after that, your doctor can extend your leave, up to 46 weeks after the birth.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, your baby receives a birth certificate, a CPR number and a name. An emergency baptism (nøddåb) can be arranged at the hospital by the hospital chaplain or the midwife, if you wish. Each parent can take up to 26 weeks of bereavement leave with benefits.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the name band.
  • To dress your baby, name your baby and let family meet your baby.
  • To understand why: a post-mortem (obduktion) is your choice, in full or in part. Results are usually discussed with a doctor, which can take several months.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge, or the hospital chaplain. Every region has a patient guide (patientvejleder) who can help you.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • From 22 weeks, your baby is buried or cremated, as after any death. The hospital gives you the papers, and a funeral director can arrange the rest with you.
  • Before 22 weeks, you can usually choose to bury your baby yourself, or let the hospital arrange a burial in a shared grave. Ask how long you have to decide.
  • A priest can lead a funeral or a blessing if you wish. If one priest says no, you can ask another.
  • Help with costs: ask Udbetaling Danmark whether you can get funeral assistance (begravelseshjælp).

Documents and your baby’s name

  • Before 22 weeks, your baby can be written in the parish register only with a burial or a blessing.
  • From 22 weeks, your baby receives an administrative CPR number, and a name of your choice can be written in the parish register.
  • If your baby was born alive, your baby has a birth certificate, a CPR number and a name. Naming is registered in the parish register, whatever your religion.

Naming your baby does not automatically give you a certificate. Ask your parish office (sognekontor) if you would like one.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Danish rules provide.

  • Bereavement leave starts the day after the loss. You can work part time during it, or go back and forth. Apply to Udbetaling Danmark within 8 weeks.
  • If you were already on parental leave, you can switch to bereavement leave with the same amount.
  • If you are still unwell because of the pregnancy, your doctor can extend the mother’s leave up to 46 weeks after the birth.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Free therapy: Familier & Sorg offers free, specialised therapy within 48 hours, grief groups and contact with other parents.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call BørneTelefonen on 116 111. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and baby loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
