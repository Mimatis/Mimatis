After the loss of a baby in Romania

Checked information for parents in Romania after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, registering your baby, the funeral, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your civil status office (starea civilă) or your pension office (casa de pensii). Romanian terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live, call 0800 801 200, free.

Four things that shape your rights

28 weeks

From 28 weeks, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn (născut mort). The birth is registered and you may bury or cremate your baby.

3 days

The time to declare a stillborn baby at the civil status office. Only a birth certificate is made, marked “born dead”.

126 days

Maternity leave. If your baby is stillborn or dies during your leave, the allowance is still paid for the whole leave.

10 sessions

Free psychological counselling for parents whose child died during pregnancy, at birth or later, under a 2024 law.

The 28-week line is about paperwork. It says nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

In Romania, the line is 28 weeks of pregnancy. From then on, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn (făt născut mort): the birth is declared within 3 days, and you may bury or cremate your baby. Before 28 weeks, the loss is treated as a miscarriage, and there is no registration.

Miscarriage or later loss, before 28 weeks

Losing a baby before 28 weeks is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you sick leave (concediu medical).
  • Time with your baby: after a later loss, the mother usually gives birth. You can ask to see and hold your baby, take photos and give a name.
  • Saying goodbye: before 28 weeks there is no registration, and the hospital usually arranges a cremation. If you would like your own farewell, say so as early as you can and ask the hospital what is possible.
  • Free counselling: a 2024 law gives parents 10 free sessions of psychological counselling. See when you go home.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 28 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 28 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Declaring the birth at the civil status office within 3 days. A birth certificate is made, marked “born dead” (născut mort). You can give your baby a name.
  • Burial or cremation is your choice, at your own cost. See saying goodbye.
  • Mother: maternity leave, paid at 85% of your income base, for the whole leave even though your baby died. At least 42 days after the birth are compulsory rest.
  • Partner: ask your employer about paternity leave and paid days off for a death in the family. These are usually set in your contract or collective agreement.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth is registered first and then the death. Maternity leave continues and is paid for its whole length. Ask the hospital to explain how the registration works for your baby.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Practice differs a lot between Romanian hospitals, so ask for what matters to you:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family. Some hospitals in Bucharest, such as Filantropia and Cantacuzino, have a cooling cot so your baby can stay with you longer.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the ID band. ARNIS gives memory boxes to some hospitals.
  • To name your baby, dress them, and have family meet them.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.
  • A priest or faith leader, if this matters to you.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor in charge or the hospital social worker. You can also file a complaint with the hospital management.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • From 28 weeks, you may bury or cremate your baby, at your own cost, through a funeral home.
  • Before 28 weeks, the hospital usually arranges a cremation. Tell the team early if you would like your own farewell, and ask what is possible.
  • The death grant (ajutor de deces) from the pension office is 4,596 lei for an insured person’s family member (from 30 March 2026). It is normally paid on a death certificate, and a stillborn baby receives only a birth certificate, so ask your pension office whether it applies to you.
  • A religious funeral is possible. Ask your priest or faith leader.
  • Ask each funeral home for a quote. Small coffins and urns for babies exist.

Handmade heart-shaped wooden caskets and urns for babies are also made by Hart van Hout.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • From 28 weeks, a stillborn baby is declared at the civil status office within 3 days. Only a birth certificate is made, marked “born dead”. You can give your baby a name.
  • Before 28 weeks, there is no registration. Many families keep a symbolic certificate or a memory box instead.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, a birth certificate and a death certificate are made.

If something is unclear, ask your local civil status office (serviciul de stare civilă).

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Romanian law provides.

  • If you are not ready to work after your leave, talk to your doctor about sick leave.
  • Questions about benefits: ask your employer or your health insurance office (casa de asigurări de sănătate).

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Talk to your family doctor or gynaecologist. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Free counselling: a 2024 law gives parents whose child died during pregnancy, at birth or later 10 free sessions of psychological counselling within 6 months, whether or not you have health insurance. Ask your family doctor or your local social assistance office how to use it where you live.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call 116 111 free. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the Day of Angel Parents (Ziua Părinților de Îngeri) in Romania. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.

Support in Romania

Where to find support

A growing number of Romanian organisations support parents after the loss of a baby, and train hospital staff.

Hospitals · Memory boxes

ARNIS

The Romanian association for long-hospitalised newborns. Its “Flori de Nu-vă-uităm” project gives memory boxes to hospitals and trains staff to support bereaved parents.

arnis.ong
Counselling

Asociația Zi de Bine

The “Mame fără Flori” project: counselling with a psychologist trained in trauma, and the first cooling cots in Romanian hospitals, with the Independent Midwives Association.

zidebine.ro
