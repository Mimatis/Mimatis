After the loss of a baby in the Czech Republic

Checked information for parents in the Czech Republic after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, registering your baby, the funeral, leave from work, benefits and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your registry office (matrika) or the Czech Social Security Administration (ČSSZ). Czech terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112 or 155. If you are in crisis, call the psychological first aid line on 116 123, free, day and night.

Four things that shape your rights

500 g

A baby born without signs of life who weighs more than 500 g, or from 22 weeks if the weight is unknown, is stillborn. The birth is registered.

96 hours

The time you have to decide whether you will arrange your baby’s funeral. Ask the hospital to explain it straight away.

14 weeks

Maternity benefit (peněžitá pomoc v mateřství) for the mother after a stillbirth, if she is insured. The father has 14 days.

5,000 CZK

The funeral grant (pohřebné) for the person who arranged and paid for the funeral, also after a later miscarriage.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

In the Czech Republic, the line is a weight of 500 g (or 22 weeks if the weight is not known). A baby born without signs of life above this line is stillborn (mrtvě narozené dítě): the birth is registered, and the parents receive maternity and paternity benefits if insured. Below it, the loss is a miscarriage (potrat). In every case, the family has 96 hours to decide on a funeral.

If the family does not decide within 96 hours, the baby is cremated and the ashes are placed in a shared grave (a “social funeral”). Ask the hospital to explain your options before you leave.

Miscarriage, under 500 g

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your gynaecologist or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you sick leave (pracovní neschopnost).
  • A funeral is possible: since 2017, parents can arrange a burial or cremation for their baby’s remains after a miscarriage. You have 96 hours from being told to decide. Tell the hospital as early as you can.
  • The funeral grant of 5,000 CZK can also apply after a miscarriage, but not usually for very early losses. Ask your labour office (Úřad práce).
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 155 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, over 500 g

When a baby dies before birth, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Registration: the registry office of the town where your baby was born issues a birth certificate within 30 days. You choose your baby’s name at the hospital.
  • Mother: 14 weeks of maternity leave and maternity benefit, if you are insured. The leave cannot end earlier than 6 weeks after the birth.
  • Father: 14 days of paternity benefit (otcovská), if insured and named on the birth certificate. You do not need to be married. He chooses when to start, within 6 weeks.
  • Funeral: decide within 96 hours. See saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, both the birth and the death are registered. The mother has 14 weeks of maternity benefit, and the father has 14 days of paternity benefit if the baby died within 6 weeks of birth. The same 96 hours apply to the funeral decision.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Practice differs between Czech hospitals, so ask for what matters to you:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the ID band.
  • To name your baby, dress them, wrap them in a blanket or give them a toy.
  • To understand why: the baby and placenta are usually examined. Ask when you will hear the results.
  • Perinatal palliative care and a chaplain, if this matters to you.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or head midwife in charge. You can make a complaint to the hospital management, and later to the regional authority.

Saying goodbye: the funeral

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. The time to decide is short, so ask for help early.

  • You have 96 hours from being told about the death to decide whether you will arrange the funeral. Choose a funeral service; it collects your baby from the hospital.
  • If you do not decide in time, your baby is cremated and the ashes are placed in a shared grave. Organisations can help you find out where.
  • The funeral grant (pohřebné) is 5,000 CZK, not means-tested, for the person who arranged and paid for the funeral. Apply within one year, online or at the labour office.
  • A religious or civil ceremony is possible. Ask each funeral service for a quote; small coffins and urns for babies exist.

Handmade heart-shaped wooden caskets and urns for babies are also made by Hart van Hout.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • Over 500 g, a stillborn baby is entered in the book of births, and a birth certificate is issued within 30 days. You can ask for the stillbirth to be noted.
  • You choose the name at the hospital.
  • Under 500 g, there is no registration. Many families keep a symbolic certificate or a memory box instead.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, both the birth and the death are registered.

If something is unclear, ask the registry office (matrika) of the town where your baby was born.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Czech law provides.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Talk to your gynaecologist or midwife. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Support groups and counselling are listed below. Dlouhá cesta supports parents who have lost a child.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call Linka bezpečí on 116 111, free. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the international day of remembrance for pregnancy and baby loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
