After the loss of a baby in Sweden

Checked information for parents in Sweden after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, documents and your baby’s name, burial, parental benefit and leave, and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with your hospital team, Försäkringskassan (the Social Insurance Agency) or the hospital counsellor (kurator). Swedish terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. For healthcare advice day and night, call 1177. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live, the suicide line 90101 answers 24 hours a day.

Four things that shape your rights

22 weeks

From the 22nd week of pregnancy, a baby who dies before birth is a stillborn child (dödfött barn).

29 days

Parental benefit for the parent who gave birth, after a stillbirth or a death around the birth.

10 + 10 days

The other parent has 10 days at the birth and 10 grief days (sorgedagar).

25 years

A grave, burial or cremation and transport are paid for by the burial fee, at no cost to you.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

Swedish law draws one line in pregnancy: the end of the 22nd week. From then on, a baby who dies before birth is a stillborn child (dödfött barn). The hospital reports the stillbirth to the Tax Agency (Skatteverket), and you have a right to a grave. This line has applied since 1 July 2008; before that it was 28 weeks.

Försäkringskassan describes the benefits for a baby who died “in the womb or around the birth”. We could not confirm from which week of pregnancy they apply, so please ask Försäkringskassan about your situation: 0771-524 524, say “tillfällig föräldrapenning”.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your midwife or gynaecologist will explain what is happening and your options. You can also talk to a counsellor or psychologist (kurator or psykolog) at the midwife clinic or the gynaecology clinic. Support is also offered to partners.
  • If you need time to recover, a doctor can sign you off sick. You can then receive sick pay (sjukpenning) from Försäkringskassan.
  • A burial is possible. By law, a grave is provided if the mother asks for it and a doctor’s certificate is given to the cemetery authority. Otherwise the hospital and the funeral authority agree on what happens; in many places the remains are cremated and the ashes laid in a memorial grove (minneslund). Tell the hospital what you would like.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavier or lasts longer than expected, if you have strong pain that painkillers do not help, or if you have a fever. From week 22+0, go straight to a maternity ward (förlossningsavdelning).

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from the 22nd week, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Parent who gave birth: 29 days of parental benefit (föräldrapenning) after the birth, then 10 grief days (sorgedagar, paid as tillfällig föräldrapenning), to be taken within 90 days of the birth.
  • Other parent: 10 days when the baby is born, then 10 grief days, also within 90 days of the birth.
  • The amount depends on your income. Some collective agreements give extra paid leave: ask your employer.
  • You do not need to apply first. Försäkringskassan is informed, sends you a letter about what you can receive, and a caseworker calls you and can help with the applications. If your baby has no personal identity number, the hospital counsellor usually contacts Försäkringskassan.
  • A grave at no cost: see burial and funerals.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are registered, as for any child. Your baby receives a personal identity number (personnummer) and you can register a name. This also applies before 22 weeks: a birth certificate and then a death certificate are issued. If your baby died around the birth, the benefits are the same as after a stillbirth. If a child under 18 dies later, each parent can receive 10 grief days within 90 days of the death. People who live with one of the parents can also receive grief days.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. In a national survey by the National Board of Health and Welfare (Socialstyrelsen), almost all Swedish maternity units said parents can stay with their baby as long as they need. What units commonly offer:

  • Time with your baby, for as long as you need. Relatives can be invited to see and hold the baby.
  • Keepsakes: casts of hands and feet, a lock of hair and photographs are offered almost everywhere. Most units give the memory bag (Minnespåsen) from Spädbarnsfonden.
  • A naming ceremony is offered in about half of units. You can ask for one.
  • Taking your baby home for a while is possible: every unit in the survey had a cool box for this, but fewer than half offered it to all families. Ask if you would like to.
  • A counsellor (kurator) to talk to, often also before the birth. Counsellors often help with contact with a funeral home and with Försäkringskassan, and contact can continue after you go home.
  • To understand why, the hospital may suggest examinations, for example of your baby and the placenta. Ask what is suggested, why, and when you will hear the results. You can take time to decide.
  • A psychologist: in the survey, few parents were offered one. If you would like to talk to a psychologist, ask for it.
  • Faith and ceremonies: the hospital church (sjukhuskyrkan) can help, for example with an emergency baptism if your baby is critically ill. Arrangements can be made for other faith traditions too.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Hospital practice differs. If you feel unheard, ask to speak with the midwife in charge, the counsellor or the head of the unit. You can also contact the patients’ advisory committee (patientnämnden) of your region, or make a complaint to the Health and Social Care Inspectorate (IVO).

Saying goodbye: burial and funerals

There is no rush to decide; take the time you need to think about what you want. You can choose burial or cremation, with a ceremony that fits your beliefs, or none.

  • Free of charge: everyone registered in Sweden pays a burial fee (begravningsavgift) through their taxes. For a stillborn baby from week 22, it covers a grave for 25 years, the burial, cremation, transport, a room to keep and see your baby, and a room for a ceremony without religious symbols. The grave is provided where the mother is registered.
  • Other costs, such as a coffin, flowers or a funeral home’s services, are not covered. Ask for a written quote. If you have a low income, contact social services (socialtjänsten) about help with funeral costs (begravningshjälp).
  • Timing: the law says the burial or cremation should happen as soon as possible and within one month. The Tax Agency can give more time if there are special reasons.
  • Before 22 weeks: a grave is provided if the mother asks and a doctor’s certificate is given. See miscarriage.
  • Spädbarnsfonden lends a pall cover (bårtäcke) for a baby’s coffin free of charge. Some municipalities have a child insurance that pays out when a child born alive dies: the hospital counsellor can check.
  • Some cemeteries have a special area for children’s graves. You can also choose another place, for example near a relative.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • After a stillbirth from week 22, the hospital reports the birth to the Tax Agency: the date of birth and death, your baby’s sex, and the mother’s details.
  • A stillborn baby is never entered in the population register (folkbokföring) and does not receive a personal identity number. Since 21 February 2022, the Tax Agency also no longer records a name for a stillborn baby. Many parents find this painful.
  • You can still give your baby a name and use it in every way that matters to you: at a naming ceremony in hospital, at the funeral, on the grave and in your family.
  • If your baby was born alive, the birth and the death are registered as for any child, and you can register the name you choose. The hospital counsellor can help you with this.

If something is unclear, ask the hospital counsellor or the Tax Agency (skatteverket.se).

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. Here is what Försäkringskassan describes. The amount of each benefit depends on your income.

  • Collective agreements sometimes give extra paid leave. Ask your employer or your union.
  • Deadlines and exact days: call Försäkringskassan on 0771-524 524 (Monday to Friday 8–16) and say “tillfällig föräldrapenning” to reach the team that supports parents who have lost a child.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in. Ask your midwife clinic (barnmorskemottagning) for advice. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Follow-up: the hospital offers a follow-up visit to go through what happened and any test results. When it happens differs between hospitals, from about 10 days to 2 months after you go home. If you have not heard anything, call the unit.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Talking together about how each of you feels can help. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words and including them in remembering usually help more than silence. Children can call or text BRIS on 116 111, day and night. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and infant loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm. Spädbarnsfonden also holds memorial services, for example around All Saints’ Day.

Where to find support

Spädbarnsfonden is the Swedish organisation for parents whose baby has died. Hospitals have counsellors, and national helplines answer day and night.
