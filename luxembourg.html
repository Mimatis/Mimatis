After the loss of a baby in Luxembourg

Checked information for parents in Luxembourg after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, your baby’s name, the funeral, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your commune’s civil registry or the CNS. French terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you need someone to talk to, SOS Détresse answers 45 45 45 every day from 11:00 to 23:00.

Four things that shape your rights

22 weeks

From 22 weeks of pregnancy, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn. Before that, the loss counts as a miscarriage.

A name

An acte d’enfant sans vie records your baby’s name, sex and dates. It can also be drawn up later.

Maternity

After a stillbirth from 22 weeks, the mother is entitled to maternity leave. Ask the CNS for how long in your case.

5 days

Special leave for the death of a minor child. Ask your employer whether it applies to you.

Your situation, step by step

In Luxembourg, a baby born without signs of life from 22 weeks of pregnancy is stillborn (enfant mort-né). A loss before that is a miscarriage (fausse couche). If your baby showed any sign of life, at any stage, your baby was born alive.

Luxembourg has no special leave after a miscarriage. The question has been raised in parliament.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you sick leave.
  • Saying goodbye: ask the hospital what is possible for your baby, including a burial, and tell them what you would like.
  • Your baby’s name: ask your commune whether an acte d’enfant sans vie is possible in your case.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 22 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Registration: the civil registry of the commune where your baby was born draws up an acte d’enfant sans vie, with your baby’s name, sex and dates (Civil Code, art. 97-1).
  • A funeral: a funeral director can help you arrange a burial or cremation. See saying goodbye.
  • Mother: maternity leave applies after a stillbirth from 22 weeks. Ask the CNS how it is calculated in your case.
  • Partner: ask your employer about paternity leave and special leave.
  • Funeral allowance: the CNS pays part of the funeral costs for a stillborn baby (20% of the full allowance). Send the paid invoices and the certificate to your health fund.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are both registered. Maternity leave applies, and parents have 5 days of special leave for the death of a minor child. The CNS funeral allowance can be claimed.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the name band.
  • To name your baby, dress them, and have family meet them.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge, or the hospital psychologist. You can also contact the national health mediation service (Service national d’information et de médiation santé).

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • A funeral director can arrange a burial or cremation with you, and a religious or civil ceremony.
  • Some cemeteries have areas for babies; the Merl cemetery in Luxembourg City has a memorial area. Ask your commune.
  • Costs: the CNS refunds part of the funeral costs for a stillborn baby, and the full allowance for a baby born alive.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • From 22 weeks, an acte d’enfant sans vie records your baby’s name, sex and dates. It is entered in the death registers.
  • Later: the acte can also be drawn up years afterwards. Ask the civil registry.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, the birth (within 5 days) and the death are both registered.

Some parents have found it hard to get the right certificates. If something is unclear, ask the civil registry, or Eidel Äerm for advice.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what applies in Luxembourg.

  • Special leave (congé extraordinaire) for the death of a minor child is 5 days, paid by your employer. Your collective agreement may give more.
  • Questions about maternity leave after a stillbirth: ask the CNS on (+352) 2757-1.
  • If you are not ready to work after your leave, talk to your doctor.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Talk to other parents: Eidel Äerm meets at Initiativ Liewensufank in Itzig, and the Red Cross runs Weesen-Elteren in Luxembourg City.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Omega 90 also supports children. Children can call the Kanner-Jugendtelefon on 116 111. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and baby loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
