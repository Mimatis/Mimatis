After the loss of a baby in Lithuania

Information for parents in Lithuania after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: what happens in hospital, time with your baby, creating memories, care for your body and mind, and organisations that can help.

Not legal or medical advice. Hospital practice varies, so please confirm anything important with your hospital team or family doctor.

Three things that shape what happens

22 weeks

From 22 weeks or a weight of 500 g, a baby who dies is counted as stillborn and must be registered.

2.6

per 1,000 births: the stillbirth rate in Lithuania (2021).

Free

Medical care in pregnancy, at birth and after birth is fully paid for by the State.

Your situation, step by step

Late miscarriage, before 22 weeks

  • No birth or death certificate is issued.
  • There is no legal requirement for burial or cremation.
  • The hospital may offer a collective burial or cremation, together with other babies.
  • You can choose to arrange a burial or cremation yourselves. Tell the hospital team before you leave.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

  • A doctor issues a medical death notice, which you use to arrange the funeral.
  • A birth or death certificate is not issued for a stillborn baby, only the medical death notice. Unlike some countries, Lithuania has no separate stillbirth certificate.
  • You can ask for an autopsy to try to understand why your baby died. It is only done with your consent.

Seeing and holding your baby

In Lithuania it is less common for parents to see or hold their baby after a stillbirth or pregnancy loss. Many families are not encouraged to, and some hospitals do not offer it as a standard option. In countries such as the Netherlands, the United Kingdom and Australia, time with the baby is considered part of good care.

This choice is available to you. If you wish to see or hold your baby, you can ask your healthcare team. You do not have to, but many parents later say that this time helped them in their grief and gave them a moment to meet their child before saying goodbye. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Hospital rules differ: some are more flexible about time with your baby, others stricter. You can:

  • Ask to speak with the hospital social worker.
  • Ask to speak with the head of department or the patient rights representative.
  • Contact a support organisation, such as Angel Mothers or the Crisis Pregnancy Center, for guidance.

Creating memories

Hospitals in Lithuania may not suggest these things, but you can ask for them:

  • Photos of your baby
  • Hand and foot prints
  • Keepsakes: a lock of hair, clothes, a blanket, the hospital bracelet, the ultrasound images
  • Time with your baby, to create memories in your own way

There is no right way to remember your baby. Some families later plant a seed letter or a flower in the memorial garden.

Care for you after the birth

  • Free care: medical care in pregnancy, at birth and after birth is fully financed by the State.
  • Your emotional well-being: since 1 July 2024, family doctors must assess how mothers are feeling after birth, using a short questionnaire (the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale). This also applies after a loss. It is a good moment to say how you really are, and to ask for psychological support.
  • Your body may react as after any birth, including milk coming in. Ask before you leave hospital what to expect, and see your doctor if anything worries you.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. The organisations below support fathers and families as well.

Saying goodbye

You can arrange a burial or cremation through any funeral service, at any stage of pregnancy. Ask for a written quote, and bring clothes, a blanket or a small toy if you wish. Two well-known services:

  • Rimtis · rimtis.lt
  • Vilniaus laidojimo rūmai “Ritualas”, Vilnius · vlr.lt

Handmade heart-shaped wooden caskets and urns for babies are also made by Hart van Hout.
