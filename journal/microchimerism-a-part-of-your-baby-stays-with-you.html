Research, explained

A part of your baby stays with you

During pregnancy, a few of a baby’s cells cross into the mother’s body, and some can stay for decades, also after a loss. What scientists have found about microchimerism, what they do not know yet, and what it can mean for parents.

4 min read 20 November 2026 Research, explained MIMATIS Journal

When you are pregnant, your body and your baby’s body share more than you might think.

During pregnancy, a small number of your baby’s cells cross into your body. Some of them stay. Scientists have found them years later, even decades later.

This is called microchimerism. It is a long word for a quiet idea: a part of your baby can stay with you.

This article explains what microchimerism is, what scientists have found, and what they do not know yet. It is written to be read slowly, or listened to.

What the word means

The word comes from the chimera, a creature in old Greek stories made of different animals. “Micro” means very small.

So microchimerism means having a very small number of cells in your body that came from another person.

During pregnancy, cells pass between mother and baby through the placenta. When a baby’s cells stay in the mother’s body after pregnancy, scientists call it fetal microchimerism.

The cells are very few. You cannot feel them, and you cannot see them. Scientists find them with special laboratory tests.

The cells can stay for decades

In 1996, Diana Bianchi and her team in the United States looked at the blood of women who had given birth to sons. In six of eight women, they found cells that came from their sons. One woman had given birth to her son 27 years before.

Later studies looked beyond the blood. In 2004, Bianchi’s team, with Kiarash Khosrotehrani, found cells from sons in tissue samples taken from women who had been pregnant with boys.

They have been found in the brain

In 2012, a team at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center in Seattle studied the brains of 59 women after they died. They found male DNA, most likely from a son carried in pregnancy, in about six in ten of these women. It was found in several parts of the brain. The oldest woman was 94.

“A part of your baby can stay with you.”

It happens after a loss too

A baby does not have to be born alive to leave cells behind.

In 2012, another research team followed 75 women during care for a miscarriage or a termination of pregnancy. They took blood before and shortly after treatment. Before treatment, fetal cells were found in 4 of the 75 women. Shortly after, they were found in 18 of them.

So a baby who dies during pregnancy can leave cells in their mother’s body too.

What we do not know yet

This is a young field of research, and many questions are still open.

  • Not every mother keeps these cells. The studies found them in many women, but not in all.
  • Most tests only find cells from boys. Scientists look for the Y chromosome, which only boys have. So cells from daughters are much harder to find, and are often missed.
  • The studies are small. The loss study had only 75 women, and it measured the cells shortly after treatment, not years later.
  • We do not know what the cells do. The authors of the brain study wrote that the meaning of these cells in the brain still needs more research. Studies of other kinds of microchimerism have linked it to some diseases, sometimes for better and sometimes for worse.

MIMATIS does not give medical advice. If you have questions about your own health, please talk to your doctor.

What it can mean for you

Science cannot tell us what a mother feels. But for many parents, this knowledge brings a quiet comfort.

Your baby was real. Your baby grew inside you, and your bodies were connected. For some mothers, it may still be so, in a way no one can see.

If your baby died before birth, at birth or soon after, you may have very few things to hold. A photo, a footprint, a name. Microchimerism reminds us that the bond between a mother and her baby is not only in memory. It may also be written, in a very small way, in the body.

You do not need science to know your baby mattered. But sometimes it helps to hear that the connection you feel has a place in the world, too.
