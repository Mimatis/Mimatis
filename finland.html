After the loss of a baby in Finland

Checked information for parents in Finland after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, your baby’s name, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your parish or Kela. Finnish terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you need someone to talk to, the MIELI crisis line 09 2525 0111 is open day and night.

Four things that shape your rights

22 weeks

From 22 weeks of pregnancy, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn, and Kela’s allowances for a birth apply.

105 days

The birth parent can receive pregnancy and parental allowance for 105 working days in a row after a stillbirth.

25 days

The other parent can receive parental allowance for up to 25 working days.

12 weeks

Hospitals and parishes should give every baby from 12 weeks a dignified burial. You can also arrange your own.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

In Finland, the line is 22 weeks of pregnancy (154 days). A baby born without signs of life from 22 weeks is stillborn (kuolleena syntynyt). A loss before that is a miscarriage (keskenmeno). If your baby showed any sign of life, at any stage, your baby was born alive.

Finland has no separate grief leave yet. If grief makes you unable to work, your doctor can give you sick leave.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you sick leave, and Kela can pay sickness allowance (sairauspäiväraha).
  • Saying goodbye: you can arrange a burial yourself, as for a later loss. If you do not, hospitals and parishes should give every baby from 12 weeks a dignified burial. Many hospitals hold shared blessings. Ask the hospital chaplain what happens at your hospital.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 22 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Burial or cremation is arranged as after any death. The hospital gives you a burial permit. See saying goodbye.
  • Your baby’s name: a stillborn baby has not received a personal identity code, and the name could not be entered in the population register. Käpy is campaigning to change this. Ask the hospital what applies now.
  • Birth parent: pregnancy and parental allowance (raskausraha, vanhempainraha) for 105 working days in a row.
  • Other parent: parental allowance for up to 25 working days, from the day the baby died. You must be away from work to receive it.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, your baby is registered with a name and an identity code. If your baby died during or shortly after birth, the birth parent can receive allowance for 105 working days and the other parent for up to 25. If your baby died later, before the age of two, both parents can receive parental allowance for 24 working days after the death, if they have days left.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the name band.
  • A blessing by the hospital chaplain, in the hospital chapel, your parish, a church or at the grave.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge, or the hospital chaplain. Every wellbeing services county has a patient ombudsman (potilasasiavastaava) who can help you.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • From 22 weeks, your baby is buried or cremated as after any death, with a burial permit from the hospital.
  • Before 22 weeks, you can choose the same. If you do not, the hospital and the parish should give your baby a dignified burial, often after a shared blessing.
  • The church funeral can include your baby’s name in the prayers, and the bells can be rung if you ask. Your wishes should be respected as far as possible.
  • All Saints’ Day (pyhäinpäivä): if you ask, your baby can be remembered at the service.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • Before 22 weeks, there is no registration.
  • From 22 weeks, a stillborn baby has not received an identity code, and the name could not be entered in the population register. The burial register records the date and place. Ask the hospital what applies now.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, your baby has an identity code and a name in the population register.

Whatever the register says, you can name your baby in your family, at the funeral and in our memorial garden.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Finnish rules provide.

  • There is no separate grief leave in Finland yet. The government is studying it with family organisations.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Talk to other parents: Käpy offers peer support to families whose baby died in late pregnancy or after birth. Church family counselling centres (perheasiain neuvottelukeskus) also help.
  • Your parish should contact you about three months after the loss to ask how you are.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call 116 111 for free. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
