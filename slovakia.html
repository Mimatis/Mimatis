After the loss of a baby in Slovakia

Checked information for parents in Slovakia after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, documents, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, the registry office (matrika), the Social Insurance Agency or the labour office. Slovak terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you need someone to talk to, the IPčko crisis line 0800 500 333 is free and open day and night.

Four things that shape your rights

1,000 g

In Slovakia, a baby born without signs of life weighing 1,000 g or more is stillborn. Below that, the law calls the loss a miscarriage.

14 weeks

After a stillbirth, the mother has 14 weeks of maternity leave, with maternity benefit (materské).

Any stage

You can ask in writing to bury your baby after a miscarriage. A stillborn baby is always buried.

8 days

If you were receiving pregnancy benefit and the pregnancy ended without a birth, report it within 8 days.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

Slovakia draws the line by weight. A baby born without signs of life weighing 1,000 g or more (or after 28 weeks, if the weight is not known) is stillborn (mŕtvo narodené dieťa). A smaller baby is counted as a miscarriage (potrat). A baby who shows a sign of life is born alive, although a baby up to 500 g is counted as born alive only after surviving 24 hours.

A line drawn by weight can feel harsh. Ask the hospital to explain which situation applies to your baby, and what you can choose.

Miscarriage, under 1,000 g

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you sick leave (PN).
  • Pregnancy benefit: if you were receiving tehotenské, report the end of the pregnancy to the Social Insurance Agency within 8 days, with the confirmation from your gynaecologist.
  • Saying goodbye: you can ask the hospital in writing to release your baby for burial, through a funeral service. The hospital must do this without delay. If you do not ask, your baby is cremated by the hospital. Tell the hospital as early as you can.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 1,000 g

When a baby dies before birth, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Registration: your baby is entered in the birth register (kniha narodení). Ask the registry office whether your baby’s name can be written.
  • A funeral follows. See saying goodbye.
  • Mother: 14 weeks of maternity leave, and maternity benefit (materské) for 14 weeks from when it started.
  • Funeral allowance (príspevok na pohreb): the parent who arranged the funeral can apply to the labour office, with the birth certificate.
  • Father or partner: ask your employer and the Social Insurance Agency what applies to you.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby was born alive, you receive a birth certificate and a death certificate, and a funeral follows. Maternity leave and maternity benefit continue for 2 weeks after the death, but always at least 14 weeks in total and at least 6 weeks after the birth. The birth allowance (príspevok pri narodení dieťaťa) can be paid even if your baby lived only a few hours. The funeral allowance can be claimed with the death certificate.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the name band. Tanana offers a memory package, and Malíček has placed memory frames in Slovak perinatal centres.
  • To name your baby, dress them, and have family meet them.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge, or the hospital chaplain or psychologist. Tanana and Poradňa ALEXIS can also advise you.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • After a stillbirth or the death of a baby born alive, a funeral follows. A funeral service can arrange a burial or cremation with you.
  • After a miscarriage, a parent can ask the hospital in writing to release the baby for burial. The hospital fills in the request with you, and the funeral service collects your baby.
  • Funeral allowance: paid by the labour office after a stillbirth or the death of a baby born alive, not after a miscarriage.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • After a miscarriage, there is no registration and no birth number.
  • After a stillbirth, your baby is entered in the birth register, and you can receive a birth certificate. Ask the registry office about writing your baby’s name.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, you receive a birth certificate and a death certificate, with your baby’s name.

Whatever the register says, you can name your baby in your family, at the funeral and in our memorial garden.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Slovak rules provide.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Talk to other parents: Tanana runs a self-help group and online psychological support for parents who lost a baby.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Poradňa ALEXIS organises activities for fathers who lost a child. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call the Linka detskej istoty on 116 111. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • October is the month of awareness of pregnancy and baby loss, and 15 October the day of remembrance. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
