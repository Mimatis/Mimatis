After the loss of a baby in Italy

Checked information for parents in Italy after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: what the law says, time with your baby, burial, registering a name, leave from work, and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Rules can be applied differently between regions, hospitals and town councils (comuni), so please confirm anything important with your hospital team, your comune or INPS.

In an emergency, call 112. To talk to someone, Telefono Amico answers every day on 02 2327 2327. Samaritans Onlus answers in English or Italian on 06 77208977, every day from 1 pm to 10 pm.

Miscarriage, before 20 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • A miscarriage (aborto spontaneo) may happen at home or in hospital. Your gynaecologist or midwife will explain your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time to recover, your family doctor or the hospital can give you sick leave (certificato di malattia).
  • Burial is possible if you ask for it. The request goes to the local health authority (ASL) within 24 hours, with a medical certificate. Tell the hospital team as soon as you can. If you do not ask, the hospital handles the remains according to health rules. Ask the hospital what will happen, and tell them what you would like as early as possible.
  • Many families mark an early loss in their own way: lighting a candle, planting a tree, writing a letter, keeping the ultrasound image.

Later loss, from 20 weeks to 180 days

When a baby dies in the second trimester, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby, give a name and create memories, as after any birth.

  • Ask the hospital what will happen to your baby’s body, and tell them what you would like. Practice can differ between hospitals and comuni. For guidance, see CiaoLapo: www.ciaolapo.it.
  • If you would like to arrange the burial or cremation yourselves, tell the hospital within 24 hours. You can also ask where your baby will be buried, so that you can visit.
  • There is no civil registration. Since 2012, CiaoLapo has provided hospitals and families with a symbolic certificate that recognises your baby’s life and your loss. Ask the hospital, or contact CiaoLapo.
  • For work, the mother receives sick leave. Maternity leave starts from the 180th day.

Stillbirth, from 180 days

When a baby dies before birth from the 180th day of pregnancy, Italian law treats it as a birth (parto) for work and leave.

  • The mother has the right to full maternity leave, and the other parent to 10 days of paternity leave. See time off work.
  • From 28 weeks, your baby is declared as stillborn (nato morto) and a birth act is drawn up. You can give your baby a name. See registering your baby.
  • Your baby is buried or cremated. From 28 weeks, this is a funeral like any other, arranged through a funeral service. See saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby lived, even for a very short time, your baby is registered with a birth act and a death act. The mother has full maternity leave, and the other parent has paternity leave. The funeral follows the usual rules.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can take time, call the people you want near you, and ask questions as many times as you need.

In 2023, the Italian societies of obstetricians and gynaecologists (SIGO, AOGOI, AGUI) published recommendations for care after a baby dies before birth. Based on them, you can ask for:

  • A single room, away from the maternity ward and from mothers with newborn babies, with the people you choose free to be with you.
  • To see and hold your baby, for as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • To call your baby by name, to dress them in clothes you bring, or to wrap them in a blanket.
  • Clear explanations, in a quiet place, with time to ask questions. If you do not speak Italian well, ask for a cultural mediator (mediatore culturale) or an interpreter.
  • To understand why. The hospital may propose an autopsy (riscontro diagnostico) and an examination of the placenta. Ask what they involve and when results are expected.
  • A contact person after you go home, and an offer of psychological support.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

Creating memories

You can ask for:

  • Photos, on your own phone or taken by the staff
  • Hand and foot prints
  • A lock of hair
  • The hospital ID bracelet
  • Ultrasound images
  • A memory box to keep them in

The recommendations say that if parents do not want photos or keepsakes at first, the hospital should keep them, so that parents can collect them later. It is worth asking about this before you leave. CiaoLapo trains hospital staff through its Memory Box project, and many maternity units now offer one.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Hospital practice varies a lot across Italy. If you feel unheard, you can:

  • Ask to speak with the head midwife (coordinatrice ostetrica) or the hospital psychologist.
  • Contact the patient relations office (URP, Ufficio Relazioni con il Pubblico).
  • Call CiaoLapo on 800 60 16 60. They have helped many families in your situation.

Saying goodbye: burial and funerals

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way, at any stage of pregnancy. The rules come from the national mortuary regulation (DPR 285/1990, article 7). Practice can differ between regions, hospitals and comuni, so always ask your hospital. For guidance, see CiaoLapo: www.ciaolapo.it.

  • Before 20 weeks, burial happens only if you ask for it.
  • From 20 to 28 weeks, ask the hospital what will happen, and tell them what you would like.
  • From 28 weeks, the same rules apply as for any person who has died.

The 24-hour rule. Before 28 weeks, if you would like to choose the place and the form of the farewell yourselves, the request must reach the ASL within 24 hours of the birth, with a medical certificate. The hospital can help you with it. If you are unsure, ask the team to note your wishes as early as possible, even before the birth.

Arranging a funeral

  • A funeral service (impresa di onoranze funebri) can arrange everything for you, including the permits.
  • You can choose burial or cremation, and a ceremony that fits your beliefs, or none.
  • Many cemeteries have a dedicated area for babies, often called a Giardino degli Angeli. Ask your comune or the cemetery office.
  • After cremation, you may keep the ashes at home or scatter them, where regional law allows it.
  • You can bring clothes, a blanket or a small toy to place with your baby.
  • You are free to choose any funeral service. Ask for a written quote before you decide.

Registering your baby and giving a name

  • From 28 weeks, a baby who dies before birth is declared as stillborn (nato morto). The civil registrar (ufficiale dello stato civile) draws up a birth act only, which notes that the baby was stillborn. You can give your baby a name. The hospital helps you with the declaration.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, a birth act and a death act are drawn up, as for any child.
  • Before 28 weeks, there is no civil registration. Some families find this very painful. You can still give your baby a name in your family and on a symbolic certificate. Since 2012, CiaoLapo has provided symbolic certificates to hospitals and families to recognise the life and the loss of a baby. AILPe also offers a certificato di riconoscimento you can fill in with your baby’s name and date.

Practice can differ between hospitals and comuni. If something is unclear, ask the hospital or your comune’s civil registry office (Ufficio di Stato Civile).

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. Here is what Italian law currently offers employees.

What happenedMotherOther parent
Loss before the 180th daySick leave (malattia) from your doctorNo specific leave
Baby died before birth, from the 180th dayFull maternity leave (congedo di maternità)10 working days of paternity leave
Baby born alive, then diedFull maternity leave10 working days of paternity leave
  • Maternity leave is not shortened because your baby died. It is paid by INPS.
  • You can choose to go back earlier. After a loss from the 180th day, or if your baby dies at birth or during the leave, the mother may return to work at any time, with 10 days’ notice to the employer and two medical certificates confirming it is safe for her health (article 16, Legislative Decree 151/2001, in Italian).
  • Paternity leave (congedo di paternità obbligatorio) is 10 working days, paid in full by INPS. It can also be taken in case of perinatal death, from 2 months before the due date to 5 months after the birth.
  • Before the 180th day, the other parent has no specific leave. Ask your employer whether your contract (contratto collettivo) gives days of bereavement leave, and see your own doctor if you cannot work.
  • If you are self-employed or on a fixed-term contract, rules can differ.

For free help with INPS applications, you can go to a patronato, an advice office run by trade unions and associations (for example INCA, INAS or ACLI). Many have staff who speak other languages.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in. Ask your midwife about your options before you leave hospital, and contact her or your doctor if anything worries you.
  • A midwife at home: in some regions, you can ask for a midwife from the hospital or the consultorio to visit you at home free of charge after the birth, also after the loss of a baby. Ask before you leave hospital.
  • Follow-up: a check-up is usually offered a few weeks after the birth. If tests were done, ask for a meeting to go through the results with a doctor.
  • Psychological support is available free of charge at your local family clinic (consultorio familiare), or through your family doctor.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Talking together about how each of you feels can help.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words and including them in remembering usually help more than silence. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and infant loss. In Italy, CiaoLapo organises events in many cities and an Onda di Luce, a wave of light, at 7 pm. Read more in our Journal.
