After the loss of a baby in the Netherlands

Checked information for parents in the Netherlands after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, registering your baby, the funeral, leave from work, care at home and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with your midwife, the hospital, your municipality (gemeente) or UWV. Dutch terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live, call 113 Suicide Prevention on 113 or 0800-0113, free, 24 hours a day.

Four things that shape your rights

24 weeks

From 24 weeks of pregnancy, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn (levenloos geboren). The birth is reported and a burial or cremation is required.

16 weeks

Full maternity leave (bevallingsverlof) for the mother after a stillbirth from 24 weeks.

6 working days

The time within which a burial or cremation takes place.

Any week

You can register your baby in your personal records (BRP) at any length of pregnancy, even years later. It is your choice.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

Dutch law draws one line in pregnancy: 24 weeks. From then on, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn (levenloos geboren): the birth must be reported to the municipality, a burial or cremation is required, and the mother has full maternity leave. A baby born before 24 weeks who lived longer than 24 hours is treated in the same way.

Miscarriage, before 24 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your midwife (verloskundige), GP (huisarts) or gynaecologist will explain what is happening and your options. You can ask your GP for a referral to talk to a psychologist.
  • If you need time off, report sick to your employer. You do not have to say why. If your employer knew you were pregnant, they can apply to UWV for a sickness benefit (Ziektewet) for you. Self-employed people can receive this benefit if they are insured for it.
  • You can still register your baby. Reporting the birth is not required before 24 weeks, but it is possible at any time, however long ago it was. You can also register your baby in your personal records (BRP). See documents and your baby’s name.
  • Saying goodbye: a burial or cremation is not required, but it is possible and increasingly common. You do not need the mayor’s permission; the cemetery or crematorium asks for a doctor’s statement. Many hospitals can arrange a cremation for early losses, usually at a cost. Ask what is possible.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain that pain relief does not help, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 24 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 24 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Reporting the birth (aangifte) to the municipality where your baby was born is required, within 3 working days and before the funeral. A parent, someone who was present at the birth, a hospital employee or the funeral director can do it. Bring your ID and the doctor’s statement.
  • Mother: the full 16 weeks of maternity leave (bevallingsverlof). For employees it starts the day after your baby died.
  • Partner: birth leave (geboorteverlof, one working week) and then additional birth leave (aanvullend geboorteverlof, up to 5 weeks) apply after a stillbirth from 24 weeks.
  • Maternity care at home (kraamzorg): you are almost always entitled to it through your health insurance. If possible, the maternity care agency sends someone experienced with the loss of a baby. You can also choose fewer visits.
  • Burial or cremation within 6 working days: see saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, both a birth certificate and a death certificate are made. A funeral director can arrange the registration for you. A burial or cremation is required, within 6 working days. The mother has the full 16 weeks of maternity leave, and the partner has birth leave and additional birth leave, as after a stillbirth.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Dutch hospitals commonly offer:

  • Time with your baby. A cooling cot (koelwieg) lets your baby stay with you in your room. Some hospitals offer the water method, in which your baby rests in cool water so that you have more time together.
  • Taking your baby home until the day of the funeral is allowed. You may take your baby in your own car, with the hospital’s statement. Many parents find it helps family and friends to see the baby at home.
  • Keepsakes: photographs, hand and footprints, a lock of hair and the blanket your baby was wrapped in. Take as many photos as you wish.
  • Free professional photographs: Stichting Make a Memory photographs babies from 23 weeks, and Stichting Still photographs babies who die shortly after birth. Ask the nurses, or contact them yourself.
  • Giving your baby a name helps many parents, so that the baby is recognised as their son or daughter.
  • A social worker (maatschappelijk werker) and a chaplain (geestelijk verzorger) can support you before and after the birth, for any faith or none.
  • To understand why, the hospital may suggest examinations, for example of your baby and the placenta. Ask what is suggested, why, and when you will hear the results. You decide.
  • Follow-up: hospitals offer an appointment with the gynaecologist to go through what happened, and some also call you after 2 to 4 weeks. Ask when it will be.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Practice differs between hospitals. If you feel unheard, ask to speak with the midwife or gynaecologist in charge, or the social worker. Every hospital has a complaints officer (klachtenfunctionaris) you can contact.

Saying goodbye: burial and funerals

Take the time you need to think about what you want, within the 6 working days. You can choose burial or cremation, with a ceremony that fits your beliefs, or none.

  • From 24 weeks, or if your baby lived longer than 24 hours, a burial or cremation is required, within 6 working days. The municipality gives written permission (verlof tot begraven of cremeren) after the birth is reported.
  • A funeral director is optional. Some specialise in supporting parents. You can also arrange things yourself.
  • A coffin is not required. A wicker basket, a special box or something you made yourself is also possible. You can include music, poems, rituals or a church service, and brothers and sisters.
  • Costs: check your funeral insurance (uitvaartverzekering). Some policies include children, sometimes from about 24 weeks of pregnancy. Ask each funeral director for a quote.
  • Before 24 weeks: see miscarriage for the options.

Handmade heart-shaped wooden caskets and urns for babies are made in the Netherlands by Hart van Hout.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • From 24 weeks, the municipality makes a certificate for a stillborn child (akte van een levenloos geboren kind). If it was made without a first name, the municipality can add one later.
  • Your baby in your personal records: since 2019, you can ask to register your stillborn baby in the Personal Records Database (BRP), at any length of pregnancy, now or years later. Your baby’s details then appear with yours, for example on MijnOverheid. It is voluntary. Each parent asks for it separately, at the municipality where they live.
  • Your baby’s surname: you can choose the mother’s, the father’s or a combination. For a first child, this choice does not bind later children.
  • Your marriage or partnership booklet (trouwboekje): your baby can be added to it.
  • If your baby was born alive, both a birth certificate and a death certificate are made.

If something is unclear, ask the civil registry (Burgerlijke Stand) of your municipality.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what UWV and the government describe.

  • Birth leave is taken within 4 weeks of the birth, and additional birth leave within 6 months. Ask your employer or UWV how this works for you.
  • If you are not ready to work after your leave, talk to your GP or the company doctor (bedrijfsarts).
  • Questions about benefits: contact UWV, or your employer’s HR department.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Talk to your maternity carer, midwife, GP or gynaecologist. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Maternity care (kraamzorg) can help at home, especially if you have other children. Some couples prefer to be alone in the first days. You decide.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Talking together about how each of you feels can help. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words and including them in remembering usually help more than silence. Children can call De Kindertelefoon free on 0800-0432. Stichting Achter de Regenboog supports children after a loss. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and infant loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm in the Wave of Light.

Support in the Netherlands

Where to find support

Independent organisations, many started by bereaved parents, support families after the loss of a baby. Hospitals have social workers, and helplines answer day and night.
