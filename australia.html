After the loss of a baby in Australia

Checked information for parents in Australia after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, registering your baby, the funeral, payments and leave from work, and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Some rules differ between states and territories, so please confirm anything important with your hospital social worker, the Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages in your state, Services Australia or the Fair Work Ombudsman.

In an emergency, call 000. For grief support any time, day or night, call Red Nose Grief and Loss on 1300 308 307. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, 24 hours a day.

Four things that shape your rights

20 weeks

From 20 weeks of pregnancy, or 400 grams if the weeks are not known, a baby born without signs of life is stillborn, and the birth is registered.

12 months

Unpaid parental leave you can still take after a stillbirth or the death of your baby. Your employer cannot cancel it.

2 days

Paid compassionate leave for each parent after a stillbirth or a miscarriage. Casual employees can take it unpaid.

24/7

Red Nose Grief and Loss answers its support line day and night, for any loss in pregnancy or after birth.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

Across Australia, the line in pregnancy is 20 weeks. A baby born with no signs of life from 20 weeks, or weighing at least 400 grams when the weeks are not known, is stillborn. The birth must be registered, and federal payments and workplace protections apply. Each state and territory runs its own registry and funeral rules, so some details differ where you live.

Rules for registration, certificates and funerals are set by each state and territory. The hospital social worker knows the rules where you are.

Miscarriage, before 20 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. If you go to hospital, ask to speak with a social worker. Your GP can also refer you for Medicare-subsidised sessions with a psychologist.
  • Compassionate leave: each parent can take 2 days of compassionate leave after a miscarriage, paid for full-time and part-time employees and unpaid for casual employees.
  • If you are not well enough to work, you can use paid sick leave. If the pregnancy ended after 12 weeks, you can also take unpaid special parental leave while you are not fit for work. Your employer can ask for a medical certificate.
  • A recognition certificate: a loss before 20 weeks cannot be registered, but most states offer a certificate to honour your baby, with your baby’s name if you wish. We confirmed this in NSW, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia and Tasmania. In Victoria, Queensland and Tasmania it is free, and in Victoria you can apply at any time, even years later. Ask the Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages in your state.
  • Saying goodbye: depending on the hospital, you may be able to take your baby home for burial, choose a private funeral, or have the hospital arrange a burial or cremation, sometimes shared with other babies. Ask what is possible and whether there is a cost.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain that pain relief does not help, or if you have a fever. Call 000 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 20 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 20 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Registration: the birth is registered with the Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages in your state, usually within 60 days. Registration is free. The hospital gives you the form; in Queensland the hospital does not register the birth for you. See documents and your baby’s name.
  • A payment, your choice of one: the Stillborn Baby Payment is a one-off, tax-free payment ($4,326.57 per baby in the rates updated on 1 July 2025), for families under an income limit or getting Family Tax Benefit Part A. Or you may be able to get Parental Leave Pay, which is taxable, paid weekly at the national minimum wage and has a work test. You cannot get both for the same baby. See payments.
  • Leave from work: you keep your right to up to 12 months of unpaid parental leave, and each parent can take 2 days of compassionate leave. See time off work.
  • Burial or cremation: your baby is buried or cremated, as after any death. See saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time and at any stage of pregnancy, both the birth and the death are registered. A funeral director can register the death for you. Parental Leave Pay continues after the death of a baby. If your family gets Family Tax Benefit, a bereavement payment may be paid, and you may receive the Newborn Upfront Payment and Newborn Supplement instead of Parental Leave Pay. Leave from work is the same as after a stillbirth, and it also applies if a child dies later, in the first 24 months of life.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Australia’s national Stillbirth Clinical Care Standard says bereaved parents should receive compassionate, respectful and culturally safe care that fits their needs, with follow-up after they go home. What hospitals commonly offer:

  • Time with your baby, to see and hold your baby. If you are not ready at first, you can still ask to see your baby later, in hospital or before the funeral.
  • Keepsakes: staff can help with hand and footprints, a lock of hair, a name card, an identification bracelet and photographs. Take as many photos as you wish.
  • Free professional photographs: Heartfelt volunteer photographers come to the hospital at no cost. Call 1800 583 768, or ask the staff to call for you.
  • Taking your baby home for a while may be possible. The hospital can explain how to care for your baby’s body and what the release process is.
  • A social worker helps with registration, the funeral, payments and leave. A chaplain or pastoral care worker can help with a blessing, a naming or other ceremonies for any faith or none.
  • To understand why, the hospital will tell you which tests are available, including an autopsy (post-mortem) and an examination of the placenta, and how they may help. An autopsy needs your consent, and you can say no. Not every hospital performs autopsies, so your baby may be cared for in another hospital for this and then brought back.
  • Results and follow-up: the standard says results should be given to you promptly and explained. Ask when your follow-up appointment will be and who to call.
  • Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families can ask for an Aboriginal liaison officer or health worker, and for help with returning your baby to country.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Hospital practice differs. If you feel unheard, ask to speak with the midwife in charge, the social worker or the hospital’s patient liaison officer. Each state and territory also has an independent health complaints commissioner.

Saying goodbye: burial and funerals

There is no rush to decide; take the time you need to think about what you want. You can choose burial or cremation, with a ceremony that fits your beliefs, or no ceremony at all.

  • After a stillbirth or the death of your baby, your baby is buried or cremated, as after any death. In Queensland, a burial or cremation must be arranged for a stillborn baby.
  • The hospital or a funeral director: many hospitals can help arrange a simple burial or cremation, especially for babies born early in pregnancy. Otherwise a funeral director looks after your baby, the paperwork and the ceremony. Rules about when a funeral director is needed differ between states and hospitals: ask the social worker.
  • Costs: ask each funeral director for a written quote. If money is a worry, ask the hospital social worker about financial help with funeral costs.
  • Before 20 weeks: see miscarriage for the options.
  • Your own ceremony: you can include brothers and sisters and relatives, choose music, readings and flowers, and dress your baby. Ask the funeral director about time to see your baby before the funeral.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • From 20 weeks (or 400 grams): the birth is registered and the records show your baby as stillborn. There is no separate death registration.
  • Your baby’s name: you do not have to give a name to register, but many parents find it helps to call their baby by name. You can include it on the registration.
  • A birth certificate in your baby’s memory, marked as stillborn, can be ordered from the registry. Some states also offer a commemorative design. A fee may apply for the certificate, even though registration is free.
  • Before 20 weeks: a recognition certificate, see miscarriage.
  • If your baby was born alive, the birth and the death are registered, and you can order a birth certificate marked as deceased.

Each state has its own registry and forms. The Stillbirth Foundation has links to all of them: registering your baby’s stillbirth.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. These rights come from the National Employment Standards in the Fair Work Act, so they apply across Australia.

What happenedYour rights at work
Miscarriage, before 20 weeks2 days compassionate leave for you or your partner. Sick leave if you are unwell. Unpaid special parental leave if the pregnancy ended after 12 weeks and you are not fit for work
Stillbirth, from 20 weeks2 days compassionate leave. Up to 12 months unpaid parental leave, or up to 24 months if your employer agrees
Baby died in the first 24 months2 days compassionate leave. Up to 12 months unpaid parental leave, as above
  • Your employer cannot cancel your unpaid parental leave or tell you to come back early because your baby was stillborn or died. For births or deaths from 7 November 2025, an employer usually also cannot cancel employer-paid parental leave for this reason.
  • If you had not given notice yet, you can still take unpaid parental leave after a stillbirth. Tell your employer as soon as you can.
  • If you want to return earlier, give at least 4 weeks’ written notice, or agree a date with your employer.
  • Questions about your situation: call the Fair Work Infoline on 13 13 94. Your enterprise agreement or contract may give you more.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in. Ask your midwife or GP how to ease it; the Australian Breastfeeding Association also helps on 1800 686 268. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Follow-up: the hospital should tell your GP and name someone you can contact. Ask for an appointment to go through what happened and any test results.
  • Red Nose Hospital to Home: a free program where a bereavement support worker with lived experience helps you in the early weeks after your baby has died. Ask Red Nose on 1300 308 307.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Talking together about how each of you feels can help. MensLine answers on 1300 78 99 78, 24 hours. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words and including them in remembering usually help more than silence. Kids Helpline answers on 1800 55 1800, day and night. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • A next pregnancy: the national standard says care should take account of what happened before, with specialist and emotional support when you need it. Tell your doctor or midwife early.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is Pregnancy and Infant Loss Remembrance Day. Many families light a candle at 7 pm in the Wave of Light.

For a future pregnancy: lowering the risk of stillbirth

Not every stillbirth can be prevented, and nothing here means a loss was anyone’s fault. Australia’s Safer Baby Bundle shares five messages for pregnancy:

  • Sleep on your side from 28 weeks of pregnancy. Either side is fine. If you wake on your back, roll back onto your side.
  • Know your baby’s movements. Learn your baby’s own pattern. If movements change or slow down, contact your midwife or hospital straight away, day or night. Do not wait until the next day.
  • Stop smoking. Ask for support: it is one of the largest avoidable causes of stillbirth.
  • Your baby’s growth is checked at your visits from 24 weeks. Extra scans may be offered if growth seems slower.
  • Timing of birth: if there are health concerns, talk with your care provider about the benefits and risks of an earlier birth.
