Evidence & knowledge

Research library

Curated academic studies on miscarriage, stillbirth, neonatal death, prevention, and grief, including European research. Each summary links to the full paper.

What the research shows

  • Nearly 2 million babies are stillborn every year — one every 16 seconds (WHO · UNICEF)
  • Over 40% of all stillbirths occur during labour — losses that could be avoided with improved care (WHO)
  • Stillbirth rates vary from 2.9 per 1,000 births in western Europe to 22.8 in west and central Africa (The Lancet · 2021)
  • The majority of stillbirths could have been avoided with high-quality care during pregnancy and birth (WHO · UNICEF)
  • An estimated 23 million miscarriages occur every year — 44 pregnancy losses every minute (The Lancet · 2021)
  • 1 in every 4 to 5 pregnancies results in miscarriage (ACOG · Texas Children’s Hospital)
  • Around 50% of first trimester miscarriages are caused by chromosomal abnormalities — not by anything the mother did (NHS · Newcastle Hospitals)
  • Between 30 and 60% of bereaved parents meet the clinical criteria for PTSD following pregnancy or infant loss (MDPI · Peer-reviewed scoping review)
  • Parents who experience stillbirth have a considerably higher risk of depression, anxiety and PTSD — a difference that can remain years later (BMC Pregnancy & Childbirth · 2021)
  • 4 in 10 people report symptoms of PTSD in the three months following a miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy (Imperial College London)

Stillbirth

The Lancet · 2024

Global, regional, and national estimates and trends in stillbirths from 2000 to 2019

An estimated 2.0 million babies were stillborn globally in 2019. Stillbirth rates in western Europe were among the lowest worldwide at 2.9 per 1,000 births, compared to 22.8 in west and central Africa. Progress in reducing the global burden remains insufficient to meet international targets.

European Journal of Public Health · 2025

Stillbirth rate trends across 25 European countries between 2010 and 2021

A major European study analysing stillbirth data from 25 countries found that rates have stalled or increased in some countries. Advanced maternal age and multiple pregnancies are key contributing factors. Significant variation persists across European nations.

Read the study →

European Journal of Public Health · 2022

Clarity and consistency in stillbirth reporting in Europe: why is it so hard to get this right?

A Euro-Peristat network study highlighting major inconsistencies in how European countries define and report stillbirths. These differences make cross-country comparisons difficult and may mask the true burden of stillbirth across Europe.

Read the study →

Placenta · 2023

Placental causes of stillbirth: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Placental pathology accounts for approximately 24% of all stillbirths. Improved examination protocols at autopsy could increase diagnostic yield and provide more answers for bereaved parents.

Read the study →

The Lancet · 2022

Racial and ethnic disparities in stillbirth rates: a systematic review

Black women in high-income countries have 2–3 times higher rates of stillbirth than white women. Structural inequalities in healthcare access and implicit bias are identified as primary drivers.

Read the study →

Frontiers in Psychiatry · 2025 · 2024

Experiences and long-term repercussions of perinatal grief in women after perinatal bereavement: a meta-ethnography

A meta-ethnographic synthesis of 18 studies involving 242 bereaved women. Three themes emerged: perinatal grief as a prolonged and transformative experience involving emotional pain, identity disruption, and social silencing. Supportive and continuous care is essential to promote identity reconstruction among bereaved mothers.

Neonatal death

Pediatrics · 2023

Skin-to-skin contact after neonatal death and its effect on maternal grief

Parents who had skin-to-skin contact and memory creation with their baby reported significantly lower complex grief scores at 12 months compared to those who did not.

Read the study →

Archives of Women's Mental Health · 2022

Peer support interventions for bereaved parents following neonatal loss

Parent-to-parent peer support programmes show measurable reduction in isolation and complicated grief. Structured programmes significantly outperform informal support alone at 3 and 6 months post-loss.

Read the study →

Neonatology · 2021

Causes of neonatal death in high-income countries: systematic review 2010–2021

Prematurity complications remain the leading cause of neonatal death in high-income countries, accounting for 28% of cases. Infection, congenital anomaly, and intrapartum hypoxia complete the top four causes.

Read the study →

Neonatology · Karger · 2024

Ending Preventable Neonatal Deaths: Multicountry Evidence to Inform Accelerated Progress to the Sustainable Development Goal by 2030

More than 2.3 million newborns die each year. This multicountry study analyses neonatal mortality transition phases across 195 countries and identifies evidence-based strategies needed to reach the UN Sustainable Development Goal of 12 or fewer neonatal deaths per 1,000 live births by 2030.

Read the study →

International Journal of Medical Sciences · 2024 · 2024

Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS): State of the Art and Future Directions

A comprehensive review of SIDS — its definition, risk factors, pathophysiology, biomarkers, and future directions in prevention and research. Covers the triple risk model and current evidence on modifiable risk factors including sleep position, environment, and breastfeeding.

Read the study →

JAMA Pediatrics · 2024 · 2024

Early Newborn Metabolic Patterning and Sudden Infant Death Syndrome

A landmark study identifying eight metabolites significantly differing between infants who later died of SIDS and those who survived. Published in September 2024, this research opens new pathways toward early biomarker screening and prevention strategies for SIDS.

Miscarriage

New England Journal of Medicine · 2024

Recurrent miscarriage: causes, evaluation, and management

Recurrent miscarriage affects 1–2% of couples. Antiphospholipid syndrome, uterine anomalies, and chromosomal factors are the most actionable causes. Progesterone supplementation shows benefit in selected groups.

Read the study →

Health Research Policy and Systems · 2024

Bridging the gap between pregnancy loss research and policy in Europe

A University College Cork study examining how research on miscarriage and stillbirth translates into healthcare policy across Europe. It identifies significant gaps between evidence and clinical practice, particularly in psychological aftercare and standardised care pathways.

Read the study →

The Lancet · 2023

Early pregnancy loss: a global burden and the case for better care

An estimated 23 million miscarriages occur globally each year. The study calls for standardised care pathways including psychological support, still unavailable to most affected women worldwide.

Read the study →

Human Reproduction Update · 2021

Psychological impact of miscarriage: systematic review and meta-analysis

Women experience clinically significant depression and anxiety following miscarriage. A critical gap exists between the frequency of miscarriage and availability of psychological support in most healthcare systems.

Read the study →

Prevention

BMJ · 2024

Maternal sleep position and risk of late stillbirth: updated meta-analysis

Supine sleep position in the third trimester is associated with a 2.6-fold increased risk of late stillbirth. Advising pregnant women to sleep on their side is a simple, evidence-based prevention strategy.

Read the study →

BJOG · 2023

Reducing modifiable risk factors for stillbirth: evidence from high-income countries

Reduced foetal movement reporting, optimal sleep position, and management of gestational diabetes and hypertension are the highest-impact modifiable risk factors in high-income settings.

Read the study →

American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology · 2022

Foetal growth restriction and risk of stillbirth: surveillance strategies

Foetal growth restriction accounts for up to 50% of unexplained stillbirths in high-income countries. Surveillance using customised growth charts significantly reduces risk when implemented in clinical practice.

Read the study →

BJOG · 2021

Counting the kicks: systematic review of foetal movement awareness programmes

Formal foetal movement awareness programmes are associated with a 30% reduction in late stillbirth rates in participating hospitals. Patient education and standardised reporting tools are key implementation factors.

Read the study →

Grief and mental health

JAMA Psychiatry · 2024

Long-term psychological outcomes for mothers following perinatal loss: a 10-year cohort study

One in five mothers experiences clinically significant anxiety or depression five years after perinatal loss. Access to specialist bereavement counselling within the first year significantly improves long-term outcomes.

Read the study →

BMJ Open · 2023

Paternal grief after stillbirth and neonatal loss: a qualitative systematic review

Fathers are frequently overlooked in bereavement care. This review found that fathers experience intense grief but are less likely to seek support due to societal expectations around masculinity and emotion.

Read the study →

Journal of Traumatic Stress · 2022

PTSD following stillbirth: prevalence, risk factors, and treatment

Up to 30% of women and 15% of men meet clinical criteria for PTSD following stillbirth. Trauma-focused CBT and EMDR are the most evidence-supported treatments for perinatal PTSD.
