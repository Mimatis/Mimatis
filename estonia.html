After the loss of a baby in Estonia

Checked information for parents in Estonia after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, documents and your baby’s name, burial, benefits and leave, and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with your hospital team, the Social Insurance Board (Sotsiaalkindlustusamet) or your local government (vald or linn). Estonian terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. To talk to someone, the emotional support line 116 123 answers every day from 10 am to midnight, in Estonian, Russian or English. The crisis line 116 006 answers 24 hours a day.

Four things that shape your rights

22 weeks

From the 22nd week of pregnancy, the law treats the loss as a birth for benefits.

€320

The birth grant is paid to one parent, also when a baby is stillborn.

100 days

A working mother receives maternity benefit for 100 days in total, including days taken before the birth.

30 days

The father receives 30 days of paid paternity benefit after a stillbirth.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

For benefits, Estonian law draws one line in pregnancy: the 22nd week. From then on, a baby who dies before birth is a stillbirth (surnultsünd), and the family has rights similar to those after any birth.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your gynaecologist or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time to recover, your doctor can give you a certificate of incapacity for work (haigusleht). The usual sickness benefit rules apply.
  • Pregnancy crisis counselling (raseduskriisi nõustamine) is a free service for miscarriage, stillbirth and the death of a newborn, given by specially trained midwives. Ask your doctor or midwife. At East Tallinn Central Hospital you can book it yourself, in Estonian or Russian. See support.
  • Many families mark an early loss in their own way: lighting a candle, planting a tree, writing a letter, keeping the ultrasound image.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from the 22nd week, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Birth grant: one parent receives a one-off €320 (sünnitoetus), also when the baby is stillborn.
  • Mother: a working mother receives maternity benefit for 100 days in total, counting any days she took before the birth. If more than 70 days were already used, she still receives 30 more days from the day after the death. A mother who was not working receives 30 days. Health insurance continues during this time.
  • Father: 30 days of paternity benefit, whether or not he had already used it before.
  • You do not usually need to apply. The hospital’s information reaches the Social Insurance Board automatically, and it sends you an offer. If nothing arrives within 14 working days, contact the Board through its self-service.
  • Your baby’s name can be written on the hospital’s documents. See documents and your baby’s name.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby lived, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are registered, as for any child. The €320 birth grant applies. If your baby dies within 70 days of birth, the mother and father have the same benefits as after a stillbirth. If a child dies before the age of three and a parent has no other benefit for it, each parent can receive 30 days of shared parental benefit (jagatav vanemahüvitis).

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can take time, call the people you want near you, and ask questions as many times as you need. This is what Estonian hospitals describe in their information for parents:

  • Time with your baby. After the birth, staff encourage you to stay with your baby. If you would rather not, you can tell the midwife.
  • Keepsakes, such as a lock of hair or hand prints. You can also ask about photos, the hospital bracelet and the ultrasound images.
  • To understand why. The hospital will usually suggest an examination of your baby (lahang) to find the cause of death, and tests of the placenta and of the mother. Results can take several months; a follow-up appointment is arranged when they are ready.
  • Your body. Milk can come in as after any birth. The hospital can give medicine to stop it, and cold compresses and pain relief can help.
  • Help with documents from the women’s clinic social worker (sotsiaaltöötaja).
  • Psychological support: a clinical psychologist through a referral, or a pregnancy crisis counsellor.
  • Information in your language. East Tallinn Central Hospital has its leaflet on stillbirth in Estonian, English and Russian. If you do not understand something, say so and ask again.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Hospital practice can differ. If you feel unheard, ask to speak with the head midwife, the social worker or the hospital psychologist. Every hospital also has a way to give feedback or make a complaint.

Saying goodbye: burial and funerals

Once the hospital documents are ready, you can choose burial or cremation for your baby. You can arrange it yourselves, through a funeral home (matusebüroo), with a ceremony that fits your beliefs, or none. If you do not want to arrange it yourselves, ask the hospital what it offers.

  • Funeral grant (matusetoetus): your local government pays a grant towards funeral costs. The amount and the deadline are set locally: for example, €250 in Põhja-Pärnumaa (2025) and €400 in Vinni (2026). Ask your vald or linn, and apply within its deadline, often two or three months.
  • For a stillborn baby, the hospital’s medical death notice (arstlik surmateatis) can be used in the application, as municipal rules allow. The Ministry of Social Affairs has said that a stillborn baby’s missing registration is not a reason to refuse the grant when the parents live in that municipality.
  • You can bring clothes, a blanket or a small toy to place with your baby.
  • You are free to choose any funeral home. Ask for a written quote before you decide.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • After a stillbirth, the hospital issues a death certificate for your baby and a statement on the cause of perinatal death (perinataalse surma põhjuse teatis).
  • You can give your baby a name. At your request, your baby’s first and last name are written on these documents. Since 2021, parents can also add a name later, at any time, by contacting the hospital that issued the documents.
  • As far as we could confirm, a stillborn baby is not entered in the population register and does not receive a birth certificate or a personal identification code. Many parents find this painful. The documents above, with your baby’s name, remain yours.
  • If your baby was born alive, the birth is registered as for any child, with the name you choose, and then the death. The hospital social worker can help you with both.

If something is unclear, ask the hospital social worker or the civil registry office (perekonnaseisuasutus) of your local government.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. Here is what Estonian law currently offers. The benefits are paid by the Social Insurance Board.

What happenedMotherFather
Loss before 22 weeksSick leave (haigusleht) from your doctorNo specific leave
Stillbirth from 22 weeks, or baby dies within 70 daysMaternity leave with benefit: 100 days in total (at least 30 days after the death); 30 days if not working30 days of paid paternity leave (isapuhkus)
Child dies later, before the age of three30 days of shared parental benefit, if no other benefit applies30 days of shared parental benefit, if no other benefit applies
  • The benefit equals your average earlier income, within the minimum and maximum set by law.
  • The mother stays health-insured while she receives the benefit.
  • The father’s 30 days are paid whether or not he had used his paternity benefit before the birth.
  • Tell your employer when you plan to take leave. If you are self-employed or work abroad, rules can differ: ask the Social Insurance Board.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth. Go to your women’s clinic or emergency department if you have severe pain, heavy bleeding or a fever above 38 °C.
  • Follow-up: when the test results are ready, the hospital arranges an appointment to go through them with you.
  • A next pregnancy: East Tallinn Central Hospital advises waiting at least 6 months after a vaginal birth and a year after a caesarean. Most women who have had a stillbirth go on to have healthy babies.
  • Psychological support: ask your family doctor for a referral to a psychologist, or for pregnancy crisis counselling.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Talking together about how each of you feels can help.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words and including them in remembering usually help more than silence. Children can also call the child helpline 116 111. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and infant loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm. Read more in our Journal.
