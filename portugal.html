After the loss of a baby in Portugal

Checked information for parents in Portugal after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, leave from work, benefits and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, the Civil Registry (Conservatória do Registo Civil) or Social Security (Segurança Social). Portuguese terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. For psychological counselling day and night, call SNS 24 on 808 24 24 24.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

In Portugal, the main line is 22 complete weeks of pregnancy. From then on, the death of a baby before birth is recorded with a death certificate, and burial or cremation follows. Before 22 weeks, there is usually no certificate. Hospitals do not all handle the weeks between 22 and 24 in the same way, so ask what applies to you.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • Mother: paid leave of 14 to 30 days (licença por interrupção da gravidez), as your doctor sets, paid at 100% by Social Security. You need a medical certificate.
  • Other parent: up to 3 consecutive days off straight after the loss. A declaration from the hospital or health centre is enough.
  • Saying goodbye: before 22 weeks there is usually no death certificate. If you would like your own farewell, tell the hospital as early as you can and ask what is possible.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • The death certificate is made by the hospital, and the death is recorded at the Civil Registry. Ask whether your baby’s name can be included.
  • Burial or cremation: a funeral home can arrange everything. See saying goodbye.
  • Mother: initial parental leave (licença parental inicial). After a stillbirth it has been 120 days; check the exact length with Social Security.
  • Father or other parent: the father’s compulsory leave; the extra optional days do not apply after a stillbirth.
  • Funeral grant: €1,611.39 for a baby who died before birth, since 9 April 2026. See saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are both registered. Parental leave applies, and so does the funeral grant. A funeral home can help with the formalities.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Practice differs between Portuguese hospitals, so ask for what matters to you:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need, alone or with family.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the ID band.
  • To name your baby, dress them, and have family meet them.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.
  • Psychological support before you leave, and a chaplain or faith leader if this matters to you.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or nurse in charge, or the hospital social worker. Every hospital has a patient office (Gabinete do Cidadão) where you can make a complaint.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • From 22 weeks, your baby can be buried or cremated through a funeral home, with a ceremony that fits your beliefs.
  • Before 22 weeks, ask the hospital what is possible. Tell the team early if you would like your own farewell.
  • The funeral grant (subsídio de funeral) is €1,611.39 for a minor or a baby who died before birth, for deaths from 9 April 2026. The person who paid for the funeral applies to Social Security within 6 months, from the first day of the month after the death. You need a declaration from the health service for a stillborn baby.
  • Ask each funeral home for a quote. Small coffins and urns for babies exist.

Handmade heart-shaped wooden caskets and urns for babies are also made by Hart van Hout.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • From 22 weeks, the hospital makes a death certificate, and the death is recorded at the Civil Registry. Ask whether your baby’s name can be included.
  • Before 22 weeks, there is usually no certificate. Many families keep a symbolic certificate or a memory box instead.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, both the birth and the death are registered.

If something is unclear, ask your Civil Registry office (Conservatória do Registo Civil).

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Portuguese law provides.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Talk to your family doctor, midwife or obstetrician. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Psychological counselling is available day and night through SNS 24 on 808 24 24 24. Your family doctor can refer you for more support.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call 116 111 free. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the international day of remembrance for pregnancy and baby loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
