Support in Austria

Verified support and information

We are so sorry that you are here. Whether your baby died early in pregnancy, later in pregnancy, at birth or soon after, your baby matters and your grief is real. In Austria, babies who die before or around birth are often called Sternenkinder — star children.

This page gathers what parents in Austria need to know, in the order you are likely to need it. Please take only what you need today. The rest will still be here.

MIMATIS shares information and trusted links. It is not legal or medical advice. Rules can change and some differ between the nine federal states (Bundesländer), so please confirm anything important with your hospital team, the registry office (Standesamt), your health insurer or the Chamber of Labour (Arbeiterkammer). Information checked in October 2026.

If you are in danger, or thinking about ending your life, please call 144 (ambulance) or 112 now. The Telefonseelsorge listens day and night, free and confidentially: 142. You do not have to carry this alone.

The most important thing to know: 500 grams

In Austria, the practical rules depend mainly on your baby’s birth weight, not on the week of pregnancy. The line is 500 grams. A baby who shows any sign of life after birth counts as born alive, whatever the weight or week.

What happens in your situation

Early miscarriage

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is very common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy. Your grief deserves care and time.

  • In the first 12 weeks, a short procedure (Kürettage) is common. If there is no medical reason against it, you can also ask to wait for the miscarriage to happen naturally. Please ask your doctor about your options.
  • After a procedure, you will not be able to see your baby. You can ask for an ultrasound print as a last image.
  • If you would like, you can still have your baby recorded at the registry office with a name. There is no deadline. See registering your baby.
  • If you need time to recover, your doctor can give you sick leave (Krankenstand). Your employer cannot dismiss you for 4 weeks after a miscarriage.
  • Many families mark an early loss in their own way: lighting a candle, planting a tree, writing a letter, keeping the ultrasound image.

Later loss, under 500 grams

When a baby dies in the second trimester, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. This can be a shock. Please know that you can see and hold your baby, and create memories, just as after a later loss.

  • You can spend time with your baby, give your baby a name and take photos. See at the hospital.
  • Registration is not required, but you can ask the registry office to record your baby, with a name. See registering your baby.
  • Whether a funeral is required depends on your state. If you do not arrange one, the hospital must take care of your baby, often in a shared grave. If you would like your own farewell, tell the hospital team as early as you can. See saying goodbye.
  • Since September 2024, if your baby died after week 18, your health insurance covers home visits by a midwife. See when you go home.
  • There is no maternity protection at the moment, but your doctor can give you sick leave. See time off work.

Stillbirth, 500 grams or more

When a baby who weighs 500 grams or more dies before or during birth, Austrian law treats it as a birth in several important ways.

  • The hospital reports your baby’s death to the registry office, and your baby’s first name is entered. See registering your baby.
  • A burial or cremation is required in every state. See saying goodbye.
  • The mother has the full maternity protection after birth (Mutterschutz), with maternity pay (Wochengeld). See time off work.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby lived, even for a very short time, your baby is registered as a birth and as a death, with first and last name. The mother has full maternity protection. A burial or cremation is required. Because your baby was a person in law, a notary will write to you about a short estate procedure (Verlassenschaftsverfahren). This is a formality, but it can be painful to receive. It may help to know that it is coming.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. If you learn that your baby has died before birth, you can talk with the team about the timing of the birth and about pain relief. You may also be able to go home for a while before the birth, to think and to gather the people you need.

These are things you can ask for:

  • To see and hold your baby, for as long as you need. You decide when to hand your baby to the midwife.
  • For family to meet your baby, including brothers and sisters. Children often want to see the baby.
  • A blessing or an emergency baptism (Nottaufe), through the hospital chaplaincy (Krankenhausseelsorge), if this matters to you.
  • Someone to describe your baby to you, if you are not ready to look yourself.
  • To understand why. In some hospitals you can decide whether an examination of your baby (Obduktion) is done. Ask the team what is possible. You can usually see your baby again afterwards.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

Creating memories

You can ask the team for:

  • Photos — on your own phone, or taken by the staff
  • Hand and foot prints
  • A lock of hair
  • The cloth your baby was wrapped in
  • An entry in your Eltern-Kind-Pass (formerly Mutter-Kind-Pass), filled in by the midwife or doctor

Free professional photos: volunteer photographers take gentle memory photos of babies in some regions. In Vorarlberg, VergissMichNicht comes to every hospital, free, from week 12 of pregnancy — ask your midwife. Elsewhere, ask your hospital whether a photographer can come.

Clothes for your baby: Verein Pusteblume gives free clothing for Sternenkinder to hospitals and parents across Austria.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Hospital practice varies across Austria. If you feel unheard, you can ask to speak with the midwife in charge, the hospital chaplaincy or a hospital psychologist, or contact the patient advocacy office (Patientenanwaltschaft) of your state. A support organisation in your region can also help.

Registering your baby and giving a name

Austria gives every parent a way to have their baby officially recorded, whatever your baby weighed.

Under 500 grams
  • Registration is not required, but since 2017 you can ask the registry office to record your baby, with a name. This is possible whether or not your baby’s sex is known.
  • You need a medical confirmation of the miscarriage from your doctor or hospital.
  • The mother applies, or the father with the mother’s consent. You receive a certificate with your baby’s name.
  • There is no deadline. You can do this months or years later, also for a loss in the past.
500 grams or more
  • The hospital reports the stillbirth to the registry office. It is recorded within 14 days.
  • Your baby’s first name is entered in the record.
  • You receive a free confirmation (Bescheinigung). You need it for your health insurer and your employer. An official certificate is free if you ask for it within two years.

If your baby was born alive and then died, your baby receives a birth certificate with first and last name, and a death certificate.

To learn more: oesterreich.gv.at — official recording of stillbirths (in English).

Saying goodbye: funerals

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way, at any stage of pregnancy. Each state has its own funeral law.

  • 500 grams or more, or born alive: burial or cremation is required in every state.
  • Under 500 grams: burial is required in Vienna, Lower Austria, Salzburg, Styria and Vorarlberg. In Carinthia, Burgenland and Tyrol it is your right, but not required. For Upper Austria, official sources currently differ, so please ask your hospital.
  • If you do not arrange a funeral, the hospital must make sure your baby is laid to rest, often in a shared grave (Sammelgrab).
Your options
  • A shared grave for babies, arranged by the hospital. You may not always be able to attend, but you can visit, and bring flowers and candles. Ask the cemetery office (Friedhofsverwaltung) for the date.
  • A family grave or a child’s grave of your own, arranged with any funeral home (Bestattung). Costs vary, so ask for a written quote.
  • Small coffins and urns: any funeral home can provide a small casket (Schatulle) or an outer urn (Überurne) for very small babies.
  • Your own ceremony: music, readings, carrying the coffin yourselves, or a last look at your baby, agreed with the funeral home. A religious funeral is possible too.
Places of memory

Many cemeteries in Austria have a grave or memorial for Sternenkinder, including Vienna (Zentralfriedhof, AKH “Ort der Erinnerung”), Graz, Linz, Salzburg, Innsbruck (Friedhof Pradl), Klagenfurt, Villach, St. Pölten, Eisenstadt, Oberwart and Rankweil, and many smaller towns. The federal brochure below lists more than 50 places by state. Hospital chaplaincies and parishes can tell you about memorial services, which many hold once or twice a year.

Every year on the second Sunday of December at 19:00, families around the world light a candle for their children (Worldwide Candle Lighting).

Time off work and money

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. Here is what Austrian law offers at the moment.

What happenedMotherProtection from dismissal
Baby under 500 gSick leave (Krankenstand) from your doctor, with pay4 weeks after the loss
Baby 500 g or more, died before birthMaternity protection: 8 weeks, or 12 after a premature birth, twins or a caesarean, with maternity pay (Wochengeld)4 months after the birth
Baby born alive, then diedMaternity protection: 8 weeks, or 12 after a premature birth, twins or a caesarean, with maternity payUntil 4 weeks after parental leave ends
  • If the protection period before the birth was shorter than 8 weeks, the time after birth is extended by the same amount, up to 16 weeks.
  • Maternity pay is paid by your health insurer (ÖGK, BVAEB or SVS). Give them and your employer the confirmation from the registry office.
  • A change is being discussed: in October 2026, the government was debating protected leave after a miscarriage, staggered by week of pregnancy. It is not law yet. Until then, sick leave is the way to take time off, and you do not have to tell your employer the reason.
  • For partners: there is no special leave after the death of a baby. Employees keep their pay for a short absence for important personal reasons, such as a funeral — usually up to about a week. Your collective agreement (Kollektivvertrag) may give more days, and your own doctor can give you sick leave if you cannot work.
Benefits to know about
  • Childcare benefit (Kinderbetreuungsgeld) is not paid after a stillbirth or miscarriage. If your baby dies while you receive it, tell your health insurer, as it ends.
  • Family allowance (Familienbeihilfe): if your baby lived and you receive it, tell the tax office (Finanzamt) within one month. We know this is hard.
  • For any work question, the Arbeiterkammer gives free advice to employees.

To learn more: the federal brochure “Stille Geburt” (Federal Chancellery, 2024, in German) explains your rights in detail.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • A midwife at home: after a stillbirth, after your baby died soon after birth, and since September 2024 after a miscarriage after week 18, your health insurance covers home visits by a midwife with a health-insurance contract (Kassenhebamme). She checks how your body is healing and can support you in your grief. Find one on hebammen.at.
  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in. Ask your midwife about your options before you leave hospital.
  • Free counselling: about 400 publicly funded family counselling centres (Familienberatungsstellen) offer free, anonymous counselling, including after loss, in more than 30 languages. See family counselling in Austria.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Talking together about how each of you feels can help.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words and including them in remembering usually help. Children and teenagers can call Rat auf Draht on 147, free, day and night.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, Christmas, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal. If sleep problems, deep sadness or a loss of meaning stay with you months later, it is not too late to ask for help from a psychotherapist (psychotherapie.at).
  • Other parents who have lived the same can be a great comfort. Support groups are listed below.

You may also find comfort in our guidance for the days after loss.
