After the loss of a baby in Malta

Checked information for parents in Malta and Gozo after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, documents, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with Mater Dei Hospital, Identità (the public registry) or DIER, the employment department.

In an emergency, call 112. If you need someone to talk to, the Mental Health Helpline 1579 and Supportline 179 are free, day and night.

Your situation, step by step

In Malta, a pregnancy that ends before 22 weeks counts as a miscarriage, including ectopic and molar pregnancies. A baby born without signs of life from 22 weeks is stillborn. If your baby showed any sign of life, at any stage, your baby was born alive.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need. Mater Dei has a new Early Pregnancy Unit.
  • Leave: since 1 January 2026, employees have 7 working days of paid miscarriage leave, taken right after the loss, at full basic pay. It covers partners too, and applies whatever your gender, contract or hours (pro rata for part time).
  • Saying goodbye: ask the hospital what is possible for your baby, including a burial, and tell them what you would like.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth from 22 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Mother: maternity leave applies from 22 weeks, even after a pregnancy loss.
  • Partner: ask your employer and DIER whether birth leave and special parental bereavement leave apply.
  • Documents: ask Identità, the public registry (+356 2590 4000), how your baby is registered and whether your baby’s name can be recorded.
  • A funeral: a funeral director can help. See saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are both registered. Maternity leave applies, and each parent has 7 days of paid special parental bereavement leave for the death of a child under 18.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. You can ask for:

  • Time with your baby, as long as you need. Grandparents and brothers and sisters can meet and hold your baby too.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair and the name band.
  • To name your baby, dress them, and say goodbye in your own way, with a priest or without.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge, or the hospital chaplain. You can also contact Mater Dei’s Patient Services.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • A funeral director can arrange a burial with you, and a church or civil ceremony.
  • Babies lost before 24 weeks have been buried together each year at the Addolorata Cemetery, in a ceremony organised by SANDS Malta within the Cana Movement. Ask the hospital what happens now.
  • Ask the hospital what happens if you do not arrange a burial yourself.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • After a miscarriage, ask the hospital which certificate you will receive. You may need it for miscarriage leave.
  • After a stillbirth, ask Identità how your baby is registered and whether the name can be recorded.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, both the birth and the death are registered, with your baby’s name.

Whatever the register says, you can name your baby in your family, at the funeral and in our memorial garden.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Maltese law provides.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Talk to other parents: Little Angels is a Maltese group for bereaved parents, with remembrance events and a safe space to talk.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call 116 111 or 179. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • October is Pregnancy and Infant Loss Awareness Month in Malta, and 15 October the Wave of Light. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
