After the loss of a baby in Poland

Checked information for parents in Poland after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, registering your baby, the funeral, leave from work, benefits and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your Civil Registry Office (Urząd Stanu Cywilnego, USC) or ZUS. Polish terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you are in crisis or have thoughts of not wanting to live, call 116 123 or 800 70 2222, free, 24 hours a day.

Four things that shape your rights

Any week

You may bury your baby at any stage of pregnancy. Since August 2025, maternity leave and the funeral allowance no longer depend on the weeks of pregnancy or on knowing your baby’s sex.

8 weeks

Paid maternity leave (urlop macierzyński) for the mother after a stillbirth or the death of a baby in the first 8 weeks.

7,000 zł

The funeral allowance (zasiłek pogrzebowy) from ZUS since 1 January 2026, for the person who paid for the funeral.

3 days

The time to register your baby at the Civil Registry Office (USC) after the hospital issues the stillbirth card.

Doctors use 22 weeks to tell a miscarriage from a stillbirth. Your love and your loss do not depend on any number of weeks.

Your situation, step by step

Doctors in Poland call a loss before 22 weeks a miscarriage (poronienie) and a loss from 22 weeks a stillbirth (martwe urodzenie). For most of your rights, this line no longer matters: you may bury your baby at any stage, and since 6 August 2025 maternity leave and the funeral allowance are available regardless of the length of pregnancy and without determining your baby’s sex.

Miscarriage, before 22 weeks

Losing a baby in the first months of pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • You may bury your baby, at any stage of pregnancy. Tell the hospital what you would like. The decision about your baby’s body is yours alone. See saying goodbye.
  • Maternity leave and the funeral allowance: since 6 August 2025, a certificate from a doctor or midwife is enough, even when there is no birth record and the baby’s sex is unknown. The Ministry says this applies regardless of the length of pregnancy. If ZUS or your employer is unsure, show them the sources at the bottom of this page.
  • Registering your baby with a name is possible if the hospital issues a stillbirth card. This card records the baby’s sex. See documents and your baby’s name.
  • If you need more time off, your doctor can give you sick leave (zwolnienie lekarskie).
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, from 22 weeks

When a baby dies before birth, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • The stillbirth card (karta martwego urodzenia) is issued by the hospital. With it, the birth is registered at the Civil Registry Office within 3 days, and you can give your baby a name.
  • Mother: 8 weeks of maternity leave, paid at 100%.
  • Partner: an employee has 2 days of special leave (urlop okolicznościowy) for the birth, also when the baby is stillborn. A doctor can give sick leave if you cannot work.
  • Funeral allowance: 7,000 zł from ZUS for the person who paid for the funeral. See saying goodbye.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, both the birth and the death are registered. A funeral director can help with the formalities. If your baby died in the first 8 weeks, the mother has 8 weeks of maternity leave after the birth, and at least 7 days from the day of the death. The funeral allowance applies as after any death.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Poland’s standard of perinatal care, updated from 7 May 2026, gives special care to women after pregnancy loss. You can expect or ask for:

  • A room away from pregnant women and mothers with healthy babies, where the hospital can arrange it.
  • Psychological help, as soon as possible. Ask for it if no one offers.
  • Time to say goodbye, if you wish. It is your choice, not an obligation. It should be possible even if only one parent wants it.
  • To give your baby a name and to keep memories: photos, a footprint, the ID band, the ultrasound image.
  • Tests to understand why, for example of the placenta. Staff may secure material for a genetic test. Ask what is suggested and when you will hear the results.
  • A referral to a mental health clinic when you leave, if you need it.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your rights are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge. You can call the Patient Ombudsman (Rzecznik Praw Pacjenta) free on 800 190 590.

Saying goodbye: burial and the funeral

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • You may bury your baby at any stage of pregnancy. Parents alone decide whether the hospital takes care of the body or they collect their baby for a funeral.
  • If you do not collect your baby, the hospital arranges the burial. Some cities have shared graves for babies.
  • A religious funeral is possible. The Catholic Church allows funerals for stillborn babies who were not baptised.
  • The funeral allowance (zasiłek pogrzebowy) is 7,000 zł from 1 January 2026. The person who paid for the funeral applies to ZUS on form Z-12 within 12 months, if you or the other parent are insured. A funeral home can submit it for you.
  • Ask each funeral home for a quote. Small caskets and urns for babies exist.

Handmade heart-shaped wooden caskets and urns for babies are also made by Hart van Hout.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • The stillbirth card (karta martwego urodzenia) is issued by the hospital when your baby’s sex is known. The Civil Registry Office then makes a birth record noting that your baby was stillborn. You can give your baby a name.
  • The sex can usually be seen from about 15 to 16 weeks. Earlier, a genetic test is possible at your own cost. It is not refunded by the NFZ and takes about 2 weeks.
  • If the sex cannot be determined, the hospital issues a death card (karta zgonu). You can still bury your baby, and since August 2025 you can still claim leave and the allowance.
  • A loss outside hospital can be registered too.
  • If your baby was born alive, both a birth record and a death record are made.

If something is unclear, ask your Civil Registry Office (USC).

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Polish law provides.

  • To claim maternity benefit, give your employer or ZUS the birth record, or, if there is none, a certificate of stillbirth from a gynaecologist or midwife.
  • If you are not ready to work after your leave, talk to your doctor about sick leave.
  • Questions about benefits: contact ZUS (or KRUS, for farmers), or your employer’s HR department.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. When you leave, the hospital tells you about care from a community midwife (położna POZ). Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Psychological help is available through the NFZ with a referral from your doctor. Support groups and helplines are listed below.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call 116 111 free, day and night. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the Day of the Lost Child (Dzień Dziecka Utraconego). Many families light a candle at 7 pm in the Wave of Light.
