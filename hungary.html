After the loss of a baby in Hungary

Checked information for parents in Hungary after a miscarriage, a stillbirth or the death of a baby soon after birth: time with your baby, the funeral, documents, leave from work and where to find support.

Not legal or medical advice. Please confirm anything important with the hospital, your employer or the government office (kormányablak). Hungarian terms are given in brackets, as you will see them on forms.

In an emergency, call 112. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live, call 116-123, free, day and night.

Four things that shape your rights

24 weeks

After 24 weeks of pregnancy (or from 500 g), a baby born without signs of life is stillborn. Before that, the law calls the loss a miscarriage.

6 weeks

After a stillbirth, the mother’s maternity leave lasts at least 6 weeks after the birth.

10 days

The father has 10 working days of leave, also if the baby is stillborn or dies.

Any week

You can ask for a burial at any stage. Tell the hospital early.

These lines are about paperwork. They say nothing about your love or the size of your loss.

Your situation, step by step

In Hungary, the line is 24 weeks of pregnancy. A baby born without signs of life after more than 24 weeks (or, if the weeks are not known, from 500 g or 30 cm) is stillborn (halva született). A loss up to 24 weeks is called a miscarriage (vetélés). If your baby showed any sign of life, at any stage, your baby was born alive.

The civil registration law counts only live births as births. If having your baby’s name on a document matters to you, ask the hospital and the registrar (anyakönyvvezető) what is possible.

Miscarriage, up to 24 weeks

Losing a baby in pregnancy is common, and it is still a real loss. Many parents feel alone with it, because others may not have known about the pregnancy.

  • Your doctor or midwife will explain what is happening and your options. Ask as many questions as you need.
  • If you need time off, your doctor can give you sick leave (táppénz). Maternity leave does not apply before 24 weeks.
  • Saying goodbye: a burial is possible if you ask for it, with the agreement of the hospital department, through a funeral company. Tell the hospital what you want before any examination of your baby. If you do not ask, the hospital takes care of your baby’s remains.
  • Seek care quickly if the bleeding becomes heavy, if you have strong pain, or if you have a fever. Call 112 in an emergency.

Stillbirth, after 24 weeks

When a baby dies before birth after 24 weeks, the mother usually gives birth to her baby. You can see and hold your baby and create memories, as after any birth. See at the hospital.

  • Burial: the hospital arranges the burial of a stillborn baby, unless you choose to arrange it yourself through a funeral company. You will be asked to sign a statement about your choice. See saying goodbye.
  • Documents: a stillborn baby is not entered in the civil register as a birth. Ask the hospital which documents you will receive, and whether your baby’s name can be written on them.
  • Mother: maternity leave (szülési szabadság) of at least 6 weeks after the birth. Ask your employer about the infant care fee (CSED) for this time.
  • Father: 10 working days of paid leave (apasági szabadság), to be taken by the end of the fourth month after the birth.

If your baby was born alive and then died

If your baby showed any sign of life, even for a very short time, the birth and the death are both registered, and you receive a birth certificate and a death certificate. The parents arrange the funeral. Your municipality can help with the costs (temetési segély), and a public funeral (köztemetés) is possible if you cannot pay. The father has 10 working days of leave. Ask your employer how long the mother’s maternity leave lasts.

At the hospital: time with your baby

Unless there is a medical emergency, you do not need to rush. Hungarian professional guidance says parents should be offered:

  • Time with your baby, to see, touch and hold your baby, without pressure.
  • A private room away from mothers with newborn babies. Ask for one.
  • Memories: photos, hand and foot prints, a lock of hair, the name band and the umbilical clamp. If the hospital cannot take photos, take your own.
  • A farewell, in your own way, with family if you wish.
  • To understand why: ask whether tests, such as a post-mortem and an examination of the placenta, are possible, and when you will hear the results.

You are not obliged to do any of this. Many parents later say that time with their baby helped them in their grief. Whatever you choose is right for you.

If you feel your wishes are not respected

Ask to speak with the doctor or midwife in charge. Every Hungarian hospital has a patients’ rights representative (betegjogi képviselő) who can help.

Saying goodbye: burial and cremation

You can say goodbye to your baby in your own way. Take the time you need to think about what you want.

  • After 24 weeks, the hospital arranges the burial unless you do. If you want to choose the place, the date or a ceremony, tell the hospital and contact a funeral company.
  • Up to 24 weeks, a burial is possible at your request. Say so early, before any examination.
  • If your baby was born alive, the parents arrange the funeral. Ask your municipality about help with the costs.
  • Ask the funeral company about a cremation, an urn and a small ceremony.

Documents and your baby’s name

  • Up to 24 weeks, there is no civil registration.
  • After 24 weeks, a stillborn baby is not registered as a birth in the civil register. Ask the hospital which documents you can receive.
  • If your baby was born alive and then died, both the birth and the death are registered, with your baby’s name.

If having your baby’s name written down matters to you, ask the hospital and the registrar. You can also give your baby a name at home, in your family and in our memorial garden.

Time off work

You do not have to go back to work while you are in shock. This is what Hungarian law provides.

  • The father’s 10 days are paid by the employer and must be taken by the end of the fourth month after the birth, in up to two parts.
  • Hungarian law also gives 2 days off when a close relative dies. Ask your employer whether this applies to you.
  • If you are not ready to work after your leave, talk to your doctor.

When you go home

Coming home without your baby can be one of the hardest moments. There is no right way to grieve and no timetable.

  • Your body may behave as after any birth, including milk coming in, bleeding and pain. Ask whether the health visitor (védőnő) can visit you. Seek care if you have heavy bleeding, strong pain or a fever.
  • Talk to other parents: the Napfogyatkozás Association runs an online group for parents whose baby died.
  • Partners grieve too, often quietly. Read our guide for partners and fathers.
  • Brothers and sisters notice when something is wrong. Simple, honest words usually help more than silence. Children can call Kék Vonal on 116 111. A seed letter can be a quiet activity to do together.
  • Difficult days often return: the due date, anniversaries, holidays, other people’s pregnancies. This is normal.
  • 15 October is the day of remembrance for pregnancy and baby loss. Many families light a candle at 7 pm.
